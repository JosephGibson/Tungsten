## Recorded limits

- **Two pushed-body cases the arrival pass leaves** (`D-092`), from the probe behind it. A pusher of 1,000 times the body's mass at 120–480 px/s can still crush the body through a 4 px dynamic gate (6 of 320 slow-push cases; 41 before the pass). At 15,360 px/s a pusher spawned after the body can end past the body it pushed (17 of 512). Nothing passes a static gate in either. Closing the first took a wall-last contact order plus static softness for heavy partners in the probe, which changes every pile.
- **A soft contact carries a bounded load, so a deep pile is compressed** (`D-063`, `D-094`). A contact under a steady load `L` rests at `linear_slop + L / (m_eff · ω²)` with `ω = 2π · min(contact_hertz, 0.25 / sub_dt)`, and its push stops growing once `bias_rate · (penetration − linear_slop)` reaches `max_push_speed`. At the defaults and gravity 3,600 (example 01) a ball-ball contact sags 0.20 px per ball weight and answers at most about 65 ball weights; a floor contact about 271. An 11,502-ball pile in the platformer's pit puts about 56 ball weights on each bottom small ball: at a steady 60 FPS the bottom 128 px sits at 0.57 of its non-overlapping spacing and the pile stands 733 px tall where about 950 px would be free of overlap. It is stable, costs a 13 ms step and sleeps; this is the load limit, not the slow-frame collapse `D-094` closed. The engine's own piles use gravity 900 and see a quarter of the sag. `contact_hertz` is the lever: 60 Hz reads stacking 1.06 against 1.32 and 951 px, while `max_push_speed` 480, 8 substeps and mass by area change nothing. It needed a constant substep first: 60 Hz softens from a 1/60 s frame, so with the step bound alone the pile's height followed the frame time, and at a frame time of 17–21 ms the pile never settled or slept. Since 0.55 `PhysicsPlugin` runs `physics_step` at the constant fixed step (`D-137`), so the lever waits as a [1.x backlog](plans/1.0/backlog-1.x.md) row: it moves the physics hash and every engine pile.
- **Kinematic bodies, sensors and queries** (`D-154`, M49's crowd probe). A plain sensor tests overlaps at the step's final positions, so one that moves farther in a step than its size plus a target's passes the target unreported; a swept sensor (`RigidBody::swept`) meets what it passes, testing each substep's moves, at a pass over every dynamic and kinematic body each substep, and the kit's zones and triggers report entries alone (8.1). Queries see the last step: a body moved since is found where the step left it, one spawned since is not found, a despawned one is skipped, and before the first step they find nothing. A `Position` write moves a kinematic body or a sensor as a teleport that no contact sees and that wakes nothing; moving by `Velocity` is what pushes and wakes. At 1/60 s a crowd of 500 circles pressing on a kinematic body reaches 5.81 px into it (circle body, spawned last), 5.76 (box, spawned last) and 5.01 (box, shuffled), over half a crowd radius; at 1/30 s and above, where such a world runs eight substeps, 1.62–2.74 px. A body pinned between a kinematic body and a wall ends 19.97–21.53 px inside the kinematic body, which nothing stops, and its centre crosses a static box's face though not a tile wall's, so W13c's mover casts before it moves; pressed into a bin's corner cell, whose open faces are the map's edge, a 3 px circle left the map (the `friction` row's first cut, whose paddles now turn two cells short of the walls). The lever not taken: moving a kinematic mover's pressed chain with it in `D-092`'s backward sweep. Two kinematic bodies never touch, since the narrow phase drops their pairs: a platformer controller pushes itself out of a kinematic body that overlaps it before its move, and reads `crushed()` when a wall or a floor cuts that short (`D-239`), but two controllers, a top-down mover and a controller's own ground overlap as before, and a platform sinks into a crushed controller until it moves away. Example 01's elevator sets a player caught in its deck out of it and turns back over one under it, on its box swept by its eased path's fastest step (M83, `D-212`).
- **The kit** (`D-155`, `D-172`, `D-173`, `D-175`, M50, M62, M63, M65). The transform hierarchy propagates positions only: a child keeps its own rotation and scale, and turning a parent does not swing its children around it. Children of a parent despawned at a command flush, by a plain `despawn` or the state stack's `SceneEntity` cleanup, draw one frame after it, until the next frame's first propagation pass takes them; `despawn_recursive`, or the parent's `SceneEntity` on each child, takes them in the same flush. A spawner that is a child of a moving parent carries a `Transform` only, written by the last frame's propagation, so its spawns land one frame behind the parent (M62). Commands flush once a frame, after `post_update`: a despawn queued in a frame's first fixed step is still alive in its second, and a spawn queued there is absent until the next frame, so the kit's recipes and its determinism hash are one-step-per-frame claims; a flush per step is a Phase 7 question, since it changes the App's frame loop, and qa6 left it (M62, M63). A cue's `label` names a manifest font face that the load does not check: core's `FontRegistry` exists only after a window loads the fonts and lacks the engine font, and the harness loads none, so a misspelt face draws in the fallback chain's head with a warning; a core list of the manifest's faces would let the load check it (M63). A child that `SpawnChildren` queues draws one frame at its queue point before its first propagation, as a spawn lands at the flush after `post_update` (M63). A `prefab` cue on a subject that a game's system or the state stack despawned earlier in the frame's commands spawns nothing, since the spawn comes from the subject (`D-170`); the kit's own despawns, a `despawn` cue's target among them, queue after the step's spawns and mark the entity's descendants (M63, M65). A cue's labels outlive a state's exit by at most their seconds, since they are the kit's, not entities with a `SceneEntity` (M63). A cue reacts in the fixed step that reads its event, so an event sent outside the fixed step, as by `damage` from `update`, reacts one step later (M63).
- **Collision layers and masks** (`D-178`, `D-228`, M68). `rigid_body` data names a layer by number or by its manifest `physics_layers` name, but a tile layer's `layers` and `mask` take numbers alone, and the headless harness's `load_manifest` loads no `physics_layers` or `physics_materials`, so a prefab naming one fails its load there. A pair that does not meet still passes the broadphase's candidate tests in a layered world; only its narrow phase, solve and sweep are skipped. A sensor's own layers matter to queries alone. The kit's movers mask their casts in code only (`with_filter`).
- **Runtime terrain** (`D-228`). Example 01's 2,048-ball pit test holds its walls at its own layout, but the soft contacts let a pressed ball's centre cross a wall face in 8 of 192 swept layouts and spawn orders, 1.82 px at most, so its containment claim has little margin (`perf-runs/20261030-runtime-terrain/`). A face between two collision layers' cells stays exposed on both sides. A hot reload, an `insert`, drops a map's runtime edits. A consumer that reads a map's journal less often than its 4,096 entries reads `Replaced` and traces the map whole. The arrival pass bounds a one-way tile's contact at a quarter cell, where a new contact takes `4 · linear_slop`. A frame with an edit rebuilds the edited map's proxies and restages the pair grid's tiles, `terrain`'s 5.35 ms `physics_step` p50.
- **Boot loading** (`D-231`). A boot slice runs at least one load unit whole, so a pack of 2,700 sprites holds one boot frame about 60 ms (the `boot` row's `startup.slice_max`, 62 ms at `default` on the reference machine), as a minute-long sound's decode does; the window answers between units. A lit sprite the kit's hit flash flashes logs once per sprite ID that it carries a material, whose shader the lit pipeline does not run, though the flash shows. A raised tile layer draws among the sprites in the default base only: `extract_tilemaps`, which a replaced base calls, keeps every layer in file order.
- **The template's pause music** (`D-187`). Pause silences the run's music and plays a loop of its own, but the run's voice plays on at volume 0, so after Resume it continues where it has got to, not where it stopped: the mixer has no voice pause (backlog).
- **Scripted runs** (`D-225`, redo 7.2). The `count` probe and `ComponentRegistry::count` cover a component registered as itself (`register::<T>`); one registered through a definition (`register_with`, every kit and engine component but those) has no count, since the registry knows only the definition's type, so a game probe counts it. A script's inputs, actions and holds apply before the frame's `PreUpdate` and its probes read after render, so a probe sees the body where the frame's physics left it: example 01's wizard held at y 300 inside terrain read y 390.5. A script run's window always shows: a hidden or unmapped surface may never present, which fails every capture (`D-142`). There is no windowless command-line run; tests run scripts through `Harness::run_script`, whose captures are recorded as not written.
- **The window's monitor on Wayland** (`D-184`). `MonitorInfo` is read again when the window moves or its scale changes, and on a resize while none is known; a Wayland window dragged between two outputs of the same scale gets neither event, so it keeps the first output's sizes until a scale change. Wayland outputs often list only their current mode, which the template pads with its fixed window sizes.
- **Audio buses, voices and fades** (`D-179`, M68). Two buses, `Music` and `Effects`; a game declares no more, and a bus's volume is the player's, with no fade or ducking (backlog). The mixer plays 64 voices: with 64 loops playing a play is dropped, and a burst past a sound's limit in one frame keeps only its last plays. A replaced `AudioCommands` resource numbers its voices from 1 again and can address a voice the old one started. A queue past 65,536 commands or a lost device restarts every loop from its start and drops the one-shots that waited; while the device is lost nothing plays, and a rebuild tries every two seconds. Switching the output device and listening to the buses are owner checks (M68).
- **Spawned entities and states** (`D-157`, `D-170`, M51, M60). A prefab queued from a source (`CommandBuffer::spawn_prefab_from`), as the kit's `Spawner` spawns, takes its source's `SceneEntity` under `StatePlugin`, and a source despawned earlier in the frame's commands spawns nothing. A prefab spawned with no source, from a system or a Tiled map, still takes none unless it passes one through `PreparedPrefab::with`. An entity that takes a state's marker in its exit frame after the dispatcher's listing, ahead of its despawns, outlives the state: one queued from a state's entity by a `pre_update` system ordered before `state_dispatcher`, or one spawned at once in `on_exit` with the marker through `with`. No kit system does either; a fix belongs to the state stack, since a sweep at the flush by state ID would also take the entities of a state that replaces itself. Two instances of one state ID on the stack share cleanup ownership: either's exit despawns both's entities (`D-217`).
- **The kit's controllers and helpers** (`D-158`, M52). The platformer controller and the top-down mover cast against the last step's positions: two controllers do not see each other's motion within a step, a body or tile spawned since the last step is unseen until the next, and nothing but a moving kinematic body's push-out (`D-239`) takes a controller out of an overlap, so one spawned inside a static solid or a tile stays embedded. A controller's first step moves nothing, a loaded one's too, since its saved state leaves the first step out (M72, `D-197`); a loaded top-down mover's too, its kept velocity carried through that still step (M75, `D-201`). The platformer's air jumps (M72) take a fresh press past coyote time and come back only on the ground. On slopes (`D-239`) it snaps down a face only from a step that ended grounded and not launched, and its slide past the slide limit is a speed it keeps only while it stands on the face. A push its body's mass takes joins its velocity once, so a sideways hit moves it a step unless its input is locked; a game turns a hard hit into a `knock_back` from `pushed()`. Its shove (M84, `D-213`) moves a light dynamic body by the body's own physics, so the shover meets it a step later and a pinned body holds it, and its climb sets its `Position` at the landing its casts cleared, a teleport no contact sees, so a body that moves into the climb's path within the step is passed. Example 01's walkers press against a crate or a keg they can neither shove nor climb, as they did before. A flash overrides a game `Tween` on the same override block, and a change made to a flashing sprite's material or block is lost at the restore. A trigger zone reports entering only, as `SensorEntered` does. A `TopDownMover` without a body moves its `Position` with no cast, so no wall stops it, and a zone or a trigger zone sees bodies only, so a bodiless entity never enters one, though a hitbox, contact damage or a pickup finds it on the kit's grid (M62, M65, `D-172`, `D-175`).
- **Sprites and the kit's shapes** (`D-190`). A sprite draws its top-left corner at its entity's point, while every kit shape (hurtbox, hitbox, contact, pickup) and an aim's origin centre on the point, and nothing gives a sprite an origin of its own: a game offsets each shape by half its sprite, as the template does with `[16, 16]` on 32 × 32 sprites, and keeps its sprites one size where the kit compares points, as a magnet or an orbit does. `SpriteFlip`, `Unlit` and `Backdrop` (redo 7.9) have no scene names yet: data flips a sprite through `facing`'s `flip` or a clip set without `left`, and a game marks a sprite unlit or spawns a backdrop from Rust (`D-231`).
- **The kit's motion and facing** (`D-174`, M64). A bodiless mover (`linear_motion`, `steering`, `follow`, `orbit`, an oscillator, a knockback) moves its `Position` with no cast, so no wall stops it, though a `steering` on a top-down mover's entity moves by the mover's casts (`D-239`); a kinematic driver moves on rails, and only the controllers and a knockback cast. A platformer controller on an orbiting kinematic platform reads its velocity a step late, since `orbit` runs after the controllers to stay on a moving centre. A placer takes its target where the target's movers leave it, so a knockback, a speed cap, a wrap or a clamp that corrects the target later in the step reaches it a step late, and each placer in a chain of placers adds a step. The kit finds a kinematic body's driver by its components, so a body a game's own system drives takes no oscillator or knockback beside it. A dynamic body enters and leaves every step under its `speed_limit`, but gravity and contacts act inside the step, so its move is bounded only in free fall, at `(cap + g·dt)·dt` a step. `arrive`'s last approach may stop harder than its acceleration, an emergency stop, and with a radius larger than a step's travel it slows in proportion to the distance and settles rather than lands exactly. One kit writer per presentation channel: an oscillator's or a `Facing`'s rotation, an oscillator's scale, hides a tween's or a squash's on the same entity. A `nearest` target scans its filter's matches for each seeker, linear in seekers times matches, so turrets among a crowd wait for a nearest search on the kit's grid, which M65 left open, or a spatial query. `wrap` and `stay_in_view` judge the camera's view without its shake while `despawn_outside` judges the shaken view, until the two are aligned. A child's aim and facing read its drawn point, which the hierarchy writes after the sync, so a child aims a frame behind its parent.
- **The kit's overlap and damage** (`D-175`, M65). A hit shape on a child stands at the child's `Transform.position`, the last frame's propagation, with no sweep, so a child's hitbox can pass what its root's motion crossed inside a step; bodies stay on roots (`D-132`). Walls end projectiles only, and only tile layers and static colliders: a kinematic or dynamic body is never a wall, and a plain hitbox or a contact damage passes through walls, so a thrown blade that should stop composes `projectile`. A bounce leaves the projectile at the contact with its movers' velocities reflected and drops the rest of its step's travel. An ended projectile stands where it ended until the flush, but a homer's steering moves it from rest for the rest of its frame's steps. Zones and the `nearest` search stay off the grid: a zone sees bodies only and `nearest` scans its filter's matches. The grid's cells are 64 px, fixed.
- **The kit's damage types** (`D-176`, M66). A World holds 64 damage types; a load whose new types pass them fails, but a type past them that no load checked (an entry inserted in code, or one left without its file) gets no bit and one warning, so its parts carry no kind of it. A hit's source lends its `penetration` and `conversion` when its request resolves, so a source despawned before then lends nothing. `Struck` and the `hit` cue carry no kinds: a reaction to a hit's types before its resistances reads the hitting item's parts.
- **The kit's effects and abilities** (`D-177`, M67). A periodic hit, an effect's payload, passes the target's invulnerability window and starts none, so a burning target takes every tick inside the window a blow opened. An effect's source is an entity and not saved: a save reloads its instances with no source, and a game names each again with `Effects::set_source` (`D-200`), as example 01 does from the save IDs it keeps; one it does not name credits no attacker after a load and lends no penetration or conversion. The `effect` action on a `hit` cue runs for a hit the target's window ignored, since `Struck` precedes the resolve; a reaction that must land with the hit uses a `damaged_part` rule on the victim's side, and an `apply` list on the hit items is a [backlog](plans/1.0/backlog-1.x.md) row. An ability written in place is named `#<index>` in its list, so a cue or an offer that names it by index moves with the list. A level-up replaces pending offers, and a chosen offer's effect lands at the next step's `effect_resolve`, since `level_tick` runs in `kit_react`. A bucket drains the step after the feed fills it, since `ailment_tick` runs before the step's resolve, so a window holds a fill one step longer, and a ladder with no `needs` rung never drains. `ailment_threshold_mult` is not a well-known stat: a game declares it in its `stats` kind.
- **Data entries and the two-phase load** (`D-171`, M61). A commit checks every prefab and data entry loaded from a file, so two edits that need each other, such as a renamed tag and the prefab that names it, load only when saved in one hot-reload batch or followed by a manifest save; saved apart, each fails against the other's old file and the log says so. A manifest reload that fails keeps every previous entry and retries only at the next manifest save, since a file that never loaded is not routed. A reader that copied an entry, at a spawn say, keeps its copy across a reload; `DataRef::resolve` reads the entry now. A prefab or data reload does not check maps' objects again, as before M61. Layer 1 parses data files as JSON only, their kinds needing the game's plugins. Nearest-name suggestions cover prefab IDs, data IDs and kinds in prefab and data files only: scenes, component names, particle configs and tilemaps print none.
- **Screen transitions and mesh particles** (`D-093`). A transition's pass does not cover screen-space text, which draws after the post stack; a game fades its own text with `1 − StateStack::transition_cover()`, as the fixtures crate's transition scene does. Mesh particles draw above every sprite, so `z_order` cannot place them between sprites. At an odd window size the radial wipe leaves the center pixel at half brightness on the fully covered frame. Pixelate never hides the frame: the state changes under the coarsest mosaic. A live manifest edit of a particle mesh has not been exercised.
- **Text fallback ties and ellipsis limits** (`D-116`, `D-117`, M34). Faces load in sorted ID order, so a fresh start resolves fallback ties between faces of equal weight, stretch and style the same way every time, but a hot reload that adds or replaces a face appends it, and those ties may resolve differently until the next start; a canonical rebuild would restart fontdb IDs, which the glyph atlas and swash cache key glyphs by. An ellipsis line limit (`TextOverflow::Ellipsis { lines: Some(n) }`) counts lines per paragraph, since cosmic-text resets it at each newline; a block-wide limit waits for the first widget that needs one (W1 M2 or later).
- **Layer 1 loads each manifest alone** (`D-089`). `crates/tungsten-core/tests/integration/manifests.rs` does not know the root sets, so a shipped material that names a shader of another root would fail there although the engine loads it. No shipped manifest crosses roots.
