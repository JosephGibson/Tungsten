### 2.2 Body seams

- `Shape::half_extents()`, a circle's radius on both axes; `Shape::distance(offset)`, how far a point `offset` from the shape's centre lies from its surface, 0 inside (01's `surface_distance`). `Collider::center(position)` and `Collider::world_box(position) -> Aabb`.
- `Shape` becomes `#[non_exhaustive]`. Each match outside core (A1) calls `half_extents` where it worked one out, or takes a wildcard arm treating an unknown shape as its box. No behaviour changes.
- `physics::teleport(world, entity, at)`: `Position`, `PrevPosition` and `Transform.position`, each present, set to `at`, `Velocity` zeroed when present, the body woken; nothing draws it sliding (the register's fix). A kinematic body or sensor jumps as a `Position` write moves it today.
- `RigidBody::gravity_scale`, 1 from every constructor (`with_gravity_scale`, finite; `rigid_body.gravity_scale`): a substep adds `gravity · sub_dt · scale`, a loose body likewise. At 1 each product is today's bit for bit.
- `PhysicsBuffers` gains one boxed `StepExtra` holding the per-proxy gravity scales, and later each step's new state (coefficients, friction data, spins, contact records, swept sensors' meetings, push records), so the struct grows by one pointer once (A3). Every later step keeps its state there or in the tile cache's boxed state, so neither `PhysicsBuffers` nor `TileCache` grows inline again, and its vectors are cleared and refilled, so a steady world allocates nothing new a step. `Proxy` stays 80 bytes. `RigidBody`'s new fields break its struct literals, two in core's tests.

### 2.3 Physics layer names and materials

- The manifest's `physics_layers`: `{ "<name>": <0–31> }`, names `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`; a name or a number given twice across loaded manifests fails the load. `PhysicsLayers`, a resource the loader inserts and a manifest reload replaces: `bit(name) -> Option<u32>` and `bits(names) -> Result<u32, _>` for game code.
- `physics_materials` (`materials` names the render's, `D-058`): `{ "<name>": { "friction", "restitution", "rolling" } }`, each optional: friction finite and ≥ 0, default 0; restitution 0..=1, default 0; rolling finite and ≥ 0, default 0; unknown fields fail. `PhysicsMaterials::get(name) -> Option<PhysicsMaterial>`.
- `RigidBody` gains `friction` and `rolling`, 0 from every constructor (`with_friction`, `with_rolling`, `with_material` setting the three coefficients).
- `rigid_body` in data: `layers` and `mask` entries are numbers or names; `material` names a physics material, whose coefficients the entry's own `friction`, `restitution` and `rolling` override. Registered with the World check (A12), so a prefab or scene naming an unknown layer or material fails its load at that field; resolved at spawn from the resources.
- A tile's `material` sets its coefficients when `PhysicsMaterials` holds that name; otherwise the tile keeps today's (friction 0, restitution 0), with no error, since a material also serves chemistry (7.10). The tile cache resolves each interned material at a refresh and again when the resource's version changes.
- No world names a material yet, so nothing changes (Q1). Tile layers keep numbers (Q4).

### 2.4 Friction

- A contact gains a tangent impulse beside its normal one: `t = (−n.y, n.x)`; μ = √(μa · μb), a tile's from its material. After each normal update, in the biased iterations and the relax, the tangent impulse removes the tangent relative velocity, its accumulated value clamped to ±μ · λn. It is warm-started as λn is, by pair index within a frame and by key across frames and repairs, from a second carry holding only nonzero tangents.
- Friction's per-contact data (μ, tangent mass, the accumulated impulse) sits in a list beside the contacts, in `StepExtra`, filled only for contacts whose μ is above 0, and solved after the normal pass of each iteration; `ContactConstraint` keeps its size. A contact whose μ is 0 does no tangent work: no velocity write, no list or carry entry. A frictionless world steps bit for bit as before and allocates nothing new, so the hashes, the pins and 01's trajectories hold.
- A kinematic side's velocity enters the tangent relative velocity, so friction carries a body on a moving platform. The arrival pass lists a body whose velocity the solve changed past its threshold, the tangent change included (`D-092`'s rule unchanged).

### 2.5 Circles that roll

- `Spin { angle, angular_velocity, prev_angle }`, opt-in on a circle (`RigidBodyBundle::with_spin`; `rigid_body.rolls: true`, refused on a box), spawned with the body, since inserting it later moves the entity's archetype and reorders the solve (`D-066`). The gather and writeback read and write it.
- At a spinning circle's contact the tangent relative velocity adds its rim's speed, the tangent mass takes `r² / I` with `I = m r² / 2`, and the tangent impulse turns its spin. A rolling impulse opposes its spin relative to the other side, accumulated within ±k · λn · r, k the larger `rolling` of the two sides.
- `physics_prev_snapshot` copies `angle` to `prev_angle`; `physics_sync` writes `Transform.rotation` interpolated as positions are. A spinner's sleep timer runs only while its rim speed `|ω| · r` is under the sleep threshold as well as its speed (the timer reads speed alone today, `step/sleep.rs:479-484`), so a spinner on a frictionless floor stays awake. A sleeping spinner freezes, and a write to its `Spin` does not wake it; `wake` does. Boxes stay upright (Q9).

### 2.6 Contact events

- `CollisionEvent` gains `speed`, the closing speed along the normal at its contact's build (`max(0, −approach)`; a clamp's, the speed it stopped), and `tile`, the cell of a tile `b` as a `TileHit`. `new` keeps its four arguments; `with_speed` and `with_tile` set the rest. After a speculative arrival that speed is small: the separated branch has already removed the approach (`step/solver.rs:67-70`).
- `ContactBegan { a, b, normal, speed, tile }` and `ContactEnded { a, b, tile }`, sent at the step's end after the collision events. A pair touches in a substep when its contact penetrates or the solve gave it a normal impulse, so a speculative contact the solver stopped counts, even one restitution bounced before it penetrated (`step/solver.rs:94-114`); a safety-net clamp counts, and so does an arrival clamp, which the arrival pass applies from pairs of its own, outside the contacts (`step/arrival.rs:172-175`, `:274-277`, `:331-341`, `:362-397`). A pair touching this step and not the last begins, with its first touching contact's sides and normal as `CollisionEvent` names them; one that touched last step and not this one ends.
- A begin's `speed` is the largest closing speed along the normal the pair had since it entered the narrow phase's margin or the arrival pass's: at each build, at each arrival clamp before it acts, and at each substep's move, after the solve and the arrival pass and before the relax (`step/mod.rs:1023-1068`). The last catches a body knocked into a contact built while it rested, whose builds read no approach. The largest is carried across substeps and steps until the pair begins or leaves the margin, so an impact reads its speed however the gap closed.
- A pair's identity is its two sides: an entity's key, and a tile's map, layer and cell (its sensor identity, `step/gather.rs:54`), not the solver's pair key, whose tile half hashes the cell's centre alone (`step/gather.rs:32-38`, `:435`). So two layers' cells at one point are two pairs, and a map despawned with another spawned in its place ends the old pairs and begins the new. A map replaced or a cell written in place keeps the pairs of its cells still solid and ends those of the emptied ones, as the sensor pass does (it drops those cells' pairs, tests them again and compares identities, `step/mod.rs:1186-1190`), so a reload sends no burst of begins. The gather fills each tile proxy's identity while the record runs, as it does in a frame with sensors.
- A pair the step does not solve keeps its state: one whose sides are both immovable for the step (asleep, static, a tile or a still kinematic body), which no proxy initiates or the contact build drops (`step/mod.rs:925-929`). It keeps it while neither side's centre, box or layers differ from the last step's; otherwise the record tests it by the narrow phase at the step's end, touching while they penetrate. A sleeper so written wakes and a support so changed wakes its sleepers (`step/sleep.rs:82-93`), but a kinematic body's `Position` write wakes nothing (`components.rs:98-99`) and supports leave kinematic bodies out, so a deck teleported from under its sleeping rider ends their pair by that test alone. A solved pair touches only as solved, so an awake body leaving a sleeper below the wake threshold (`step/mod.rs:889-905`) ends their pair (Q5). A pair whose body is despawned ends, naming the dead entity (Q6). Began in first-touch order, ended in the last step's order.
- `PhysicsPlugin` registers both queues. The step keeps the record only while one of them exists, so 02's rows do no new work (A19); 01's `pit` pin moves as step 5's Moves says.

### 2.7 Sensor exits and swept sensors

- `SensorExited { sensor, other, tile }`, after the entries: each pair of the last pass this pass lacks, whether the two separated, the sensor's mask dropped the layer, the cell was written away or either was despawned (the entities may be dead, Q6).
- `SensorEntered` gains `tile` and `point`: the other's centre at the step's end, or for a swept sensor at their first touch; a tile's, its cell's centre. It loses `Eq`, `point` being a `Vec2`; `new` keeps its two arguments, and core's sensor tests compare `(sensor, other, tile)` (A23).
- The pass keeps each tile pair's cell beside its key, since a tile identity is a hash; a sensor's and a body's entity come from their keys (A7).
- `RigidBody::swept`, a sensor's alone (`with_swept`; `rigid_body.swept: true`, refused on another kind): it is tested in each substep, after the safety net, its share of its step's move against each moving body's move in that substep (`prev_center` to `center`), by the query's casts in relative motion with a start overlap counting. Its candidates are the awake dynamic and kinematic bodies whose box over their substep move meets its own, found by a pass over them, not from the pair grid, which keeps a repaired body under its old box (`step/pairs.rs:470-478`) and stages no move the solve hands a body past its allowance (`step/mod.rs:1023-1066`, `step/arrival.rs:148-153`). A body's path is a straight segment within a substep, so a crossing followed by a bounce is met (the solve, relax and restitution change velocity around each substep's move, `step/mod.rs:1023-1071`). Static bodies, sleepers and the cells under its swept box, which do not move within the step, are tested once against its whole move. Its pairs are what it met over the step, so a body it passed enters and, at the next step, exits unless met again. A world without a swept sensor runs no such test.
- `PhysicsPlugin` registers `SensorExited`; 01's `pit` pin moves as step 6's Moves says.

### 2.8 Impulses

- `physics::apply_impulse(world, entity, impulse) -> bool`: a dynamic body's `Velocity` changes by `impulse · inv_mass` at once and it wakes; a kinematic body with a mass records a push (below); a static body, a sensor or a kinematic body without a mass takes none.
- `physics::apply_radial_impulse(world, center, radius, impulse, filter) -> usize`: each dynamic or kinematic body the filter sees whose shape lies within `radius` of `center` (`Shape::distance`, the last step's positions, `overlap`'s order) takes `impulse · (1 − d / radius)` from `center` toward its centre, straight up where they coincide, as `apply_impulse` gives it; it returns how many took one. Falloff is linear (Q11).
- A kinematic body may have a mass `M` (`with_mass`; `rigid_body.mass`, refused today off a dynamic body, `plugin.rs:220-222`), its reaction mass; the gather still gives it inverse mass 0, so the solver holds it immovable (`step/gather.rs:123-129`). Its push record holds a velocity change: `J / M` for each impulse `J` applied to it, and `−J / (m + M)` for each impulse `J` a dynamic body of mass `m` took from it. At a contact, `J` is its normal and tangent impulse in each substep after the relax and before restitution, which adds to the same accumulated impulse (`step/mod.rs:1068-1071`, `step/solver.rs:94-114`), summed over the substeps; at an arrival clamp, forward or backward, of that body against it or against a pressed chain that ends at it, the clamp's impulse (`step/arrival.rs:331-341`, `:362-397`; a clamp is inelastic). So the record is the inelastic share whatever the restitution: a push never exceeds the hitting body's closing speed and a light body is never launched by a heavy one's stop. `physics::take_push(world, entity) -> Vec2` returns an entry and clears it; a despawned body's entry drops at the next step; a kinematic body without a mass records nothing, so today's worlds keep no record.

### 2.9 Casts that pass refused hits

A refused hit is passed. `keep` may read the World, for a body's kind, mass or tags.

### 2.11 Slope tiles

- A tileset tile's `slope`: `"45"`, `"2:1 low"` or `"2:1 high"`, then the cell corner its solid part holds, `br`, `bl`, `tr` or `tl` (`"45 br"` is solid below the diagonal from the bottom-left to the top-right corner): a 45° triangle, a 2:1 triangle half a cell high, or a 2:1 trapezoid from half a cell to a cell, so a low cell and a high cell side by side make one 2:1 surface. `tr` and `tl` are ceilings. A slope tile cannot be one-way (a load error). The auto-tiler counts a slope cell as any tile of its style; slope art by neighbour is 7.12's.
- The outline: a slope cell's legs are the cell sides its polygon touches; its sloped face is always exposed. A face or leg is masked when the neighbour's side across it covers its whole extent: a full face, a full leg, or an equal half leg, as a low cell's half leg meets a high cell's. A slope cell is a run of its own, and its key mixes its kind as a one-way cell's does, so a cell changing kind is a new support to the sleep record (`D-156`).
- The geometry is `physics/step/slope.rs`, inside the LLM index's `core/physics/step/` row (A22): a kind's polygon in a cell; a box's contact by SAT on x, y and the slope's normal, a masked leg never the axis and a corner gap's normal clamped to the exposed side, its gap that side's projected gap, as `aabb_vs_aabb_speculative` does (`collision.rs:137-153`); a circle's from the closest point on the exposed edges, a vertex beside a masked leg taking the exposed edge's normal and its distance along it, as `aabb_vs_circle_speculative` does (`collision.rs:372-385`); both speculative within the margin; and a box swept against the polygon (the Minkowski sum, a masked leg's corner square as `enter_rounded_box` squares one), which the safety net needs. Step 11 adds rays and circle casts, overlap and the nearest point.
- The step: the kind rides in a padding byte of `Proxy` (80 bytes, A3), `TileRun`, `GridCell` and `IndexEntry`, so none grows; `masked_contact` dispatches slope proxies to the slope contacts, so the substep, the contact build and the arrival pass take them; the safety net sweeps a body's box against a slope cell's polygon, not its box.
- Queries (step 11): casts, `overlap`, `nearest` and sensors meet the polygon; `TerrainFace` gives the sloped face as a segment and its normal, which the physics overlay draws. Until step 11 lands, queries meet a slope cell as its box; no map has one.

### 2.12 The controller on slopes

- `slope_limit` (degrees; by default today's floor rule, a normal's y at most −0.7, held exactly): a surface within it is a floor, a steeper one a wall. `slide_limit` (degrees, by default the slope limit): grounded on a floor steeper than it, the controller slides down along the floor at the speed gravity's share along it gives, its run added, until the floor is gentler.
- A grounded run moves along the floor's tangent at `run · run_speed`, so it climbs and descends at its run speed; on a flat floor the tangent is the x axis and the move is today's bit for bit.
- Snap-down: grounded and not lifted, its probe reaches `max(1, run_speed · dt · tan(slope_limit))` px; it snaps onto a floor within that reach when the floor slopes or the controller stood on a slope at its last step, and within 1 px otherwise, so it leaves a ledge on flat ground at today's step. `floor_normal()` reads the last step's ground normal.
- Drop-through: `drop_through()`, or a jump press with the Intent's movement down (`y > 0.5`) on a one-way floor, makes its probe and moves skip one-way tiles (`SpatialFilter::with_one_way(false)`) until its bottom is below the top face of the edge it stood on, read from its probe's hit, or it lands on another floor or rises; from there the one-way rule passes it (`D-228`). One step is not enough: its first fall from the 0.01 px skin is `g · dt² / 2`, 0.0014 px at gravity 10 (`slide.rs:10`, `platformer.rs:752`). The saved form keeps that edge's top while a drop lasts.
- S-99: `mask` and `without` in its data (names or numbers, §2.3, checked at load) build its filter as `with_mask` and `without_layers` do; the saved form writes them as bits when they differ from the defaults, so an older save loads with today's filter. `with_filter` stays for code.
- `slide.rs` gains the floor threshold as an argument of a new function, the old one calling it with −0.7, so the top-down mover and the knockback are unchanged (A14). The step climb stays beside slopes (Q2).

### 2.13 The controller pushed and riding

- K7: a controller whose kinematic body has a mass (§2.8; `rigid_body.mass` on its prefab) takes its body's push each step and adds it as a push, so a ball that hits it, a radial impulse and a pile leaning on it move it, by the inelastic share at most. Without a mass there is no record, as today.
- K23: before its move, each kinematic body its filter sees that overlaps its shape at the last step's positions pushes it out along their contact's normal by the overlap, unless that body is its ground or a kit mover (a `PlatformerController` or a `TopDownMover`: 01's actors are kinematic, A17), so a platform or a deck pushes it and another walker never does. The push is cast against the world with that body refused (step 8's predicate, since a filter excludes only its own body); a push a wall or floor cuts short leaves it overlapped, and `crushed()` reads true for that step.
- K30: under locked input, its body's mass `M` set, a dynamic body of mass `m` met as a wall that it cannot shove takes an inelastic hit, `J = M m / (M + m) · max(0, u − w)` along the move, `u` and `w` the controller's and the body's speeds into the wall, through `apply_impulse`; the controller keeps `u − J / M` into it rather than zero. Without a mass, or at a static body, a kinematic body or a tile, it stops as today.
- S-132: a kinematic ground's carry is moved by `move_and_slide` from where its own move ended, the ground refused, so a wall or a ceiling stops it; an unblocked carry moves by `carry · dt` exactly, as today.
- The shove (`D-213`) gives the body `apply_impulse((m · (speed − v.x), 0))` where it moves slower that way, today's speed up to rounding; the controller follows as today.
