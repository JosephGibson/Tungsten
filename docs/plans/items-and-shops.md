## 2. Design

### 2.1 8.1's second plan and its edges

The three plans split 8.1 by what each stands on (`ROADMAP.md`'s `phase-8` line, from `story-systems`' §2.1). This one builds the items and their screens; the third plays cutscenes, runs the clock and loads areas. Its edge with 8.7 follows the ARPG design's §16: 8.1 owns the instance and what it does once it exists; 8.7 owns rolling one. Two choices sit on that edge:

- **`affixes` lands here as data** (Q-b): §4.6's instance names its affixes by ID, tier and values, so showing its lines needs each affix's lines, tiers and ranges. Its `weights` and its tiers' `weight` are parsed and checked here and read by 8.7's roll; `rarities`, `uniques`, `drop_level` and §7's eligibility warning stay 8.7's.
- **The weapon block is built, folded, shown and saved here, read by no attack** (Q-c): 8.7's sheet reads (PA-3, PA-4) take an attack's main-hand read, a follow-up to Later naming 8.7.

### 2.2 Where it lives

- **The kit:** an `items` domain (`items/mod.rs`, `base.rs`, `affix.rs`, `item.rs`, `inventory.rs`, `ground.rs`, `shop.rs`), the ARPG design's §3.1 `items`. `ItemsPlugin` and `ShopsPlugin` join `KitPlugins::set()` after `QuestsPlugin`, before `KitDebugPlugin` (Q-a): every system gated on `World::has_any` of its holder (`D-247`), so a World with no inventory or ground item runs no new query (A11, A12). The pickup's gate (Step 3), the `item` condition (Step 2), the three actions (Steps 2, 4) and the apart request (Step 2) change their own modules.
- **Core:** the grid (Step 5), item drags and their ghost (Step 6), the screen file's additions (Steps 5–7).
- **The umbrella:** `screens::popup`, tooltips and popups for every `tungsten::screens` modal screen (Step 7); `items.rs` behind `kit`, `ItemsUiPlugin`, which a game adds: the inventory and shop screens and the item tooltip's bindings (Step 8).

### 2.3 Bases, affixes and instances (Step 1)

- **`item_bases`,** each entry: `name` (required), `icon` (a sprite ID, required), `text`, `tags` (declared), `slot` (a slot kind; none: not worn), `stack` (1–9,999, default 1; past 1 refuses `slot` and `weapon`), `price` (whole coins, default 0), `requires` (a condition on the wearer as `self`; `other` refused), `grant` (at most 8 declared tags, while worn), `implicit` (at most 8 item lines), `weapon` (a block), `use` (a story list of at most 16 actions, `check_list`'s rules, A8), `consume` (default true when `use` is set), `ground` (the prefab a drop spawns, Step 3).
- **`weapon_block`:** `damage`, a map of declared damage tags to `[min, max]` (finite, 0 ≤ min ≤ max), `use_time` (> 0 s), `crit_chance` (0–1).
- **`item_line`:** one object, a `skill_line`'s form (a `stat` with its `modifier` and `with`, `while` and `against`; an `added`; a `pen`, A3) or a `resist` (a `resist_modifier`'s form), and two fields of its own: `local` (default false) and `text`, shown in place of the generic text, `{0}` to `{3}` standing for the line's rolled values. A number in an affix's line may be `{ "value": i, "times": k }` (`rolled_value`: the `i`-th rolled value times `k`).
- **Local lines** are refused on a base without a `weapon` block (field path, A3). A local line takes `with` alone, naming the parts it folds into; `while` and `against`, which need a holder and a hit the build has not (A31), are refused on it. A local line's stat is one of the block's: `damage` (its parts, or the parts its `with` names), `use_time` or `crit_chance`; a local `added` adds to a part or adds one. The fold is the kit's modifier order over the base value (sum the adds, then `× (1 + Σ pct)`, then the product of the `mul`s); an override is refused on a local line.
- **`affixes`,** each entry: `kind` (`prefix`, `suffix`), `group`, `weights` (at most 16 `affix_weight`s, `{ tag, weight ≥ 0 }`, declared tags), `tiers` (1–16 `affix_tier`s, ascending by `level` 1–100, each with `weight` > 0 and 1–4 `values` ranges `[min, max]`, `step` > 0, default 1), `lines` (1–8 item lines, each `rolled_value` naming a range every tier has). The check holds tags, stats (`check_stat`), ranges and the ascending levels.
- **`item`,** §4.6's JSON: `uid` (0 until assigned, Step 2), `base`, `level` (1–100, default 1), `rarity` (a name, default `normal`), `seed` (a hex `u64`, default `0x0`, kept for 8.7's reroll), `affixes` (at most 12 `rolled_affix`es, `{ id, tier, values }`, `tier` 1-based), `name` (at most 4 words), `count` (1 to the base's `stack`, written past 1). Data that holds one (a prefab's, Steps 2 and 3) is checked at load with field paths: the base and each affix known, the tier there with `level` ≤ the item's, one value a range, each inside its range on its step, no group twice, a local line only on a base with a block. `unique` (8.7's form) is refused. A restored item is kept as saved, never refused or dropped (T5): an unknown base leaves it shown by its base ID with no lines, an unknown affix or tier leaves those lines out, each warned once; Step 2's restore hook rebuilds what a worn one applies. A build skips, warned once, each line it cannot read from the saved item, such as a `rolled_value` past the values saved for its affix or a local line on a base that has since lost its block, and builds the rest.
- **`built(world, &Item) -> Option<BuiltItem>`:** its name (its words, else the base's), rarity, block after its local lines, its global lines in order (the base's implicit lines, then each affix's at its values), each with its text: the line's own, else a generic one (`+22% damage with damage.fire`, `+1 crit_chance`, `adds 4–6 damage.fire`, `fire resist +19%`), its numbers rounded for display. Tags print as written (Q-h).
- `ItemsPlugin` registers both kinds with their descriptions (genres `rpg` and `arpg`) and nothing else in this step.

### 2.4 The inventory and the equipment status (Step 2)

- **`inventory`,** a component: `{ "cells": 24, "slots": { "lantern": "lantern", "charm_1": "charm", "charm_2": "charm" }, "items": [ "oil_flask", { "base": "oilskin_hood" } ] }`: 1–256 cells; at most 32 slots, each a name and the slot kind it takes; `items`, the bag's first cells at spawn (checked). Saved at version 1 with its declarations, since a restore inserts the saved component whole (A29): `{ "cells": [null | item], "slots": { name: { "kind": .., "item": null | item, "status": null | serial } } }`, the cell count the array's length, an empty slot kept.
- **`ItemLedger`,** the game's resource, saved at version 1: `next_uid` (from 1) and `sold` (Step 4's counts by `<shop>/<base>`, empty until then, so Step 4 moves no saved form). An item takes a uid when it is first given, worn or bought.
- **Calls,** applied at once, since an inventory screen holds the clock (A28): `give(world, holder, base, count) -> u32` (stacks first, onto items of its base with no affixes and the same level and rarity, up to `stack`, then the first empty cells; what fit), `give_item(world, holder, Item) -> Result<(), Item>` (whole or handed back), `take(world, holder, base, count) -> u32` (from the bag's cells in order, never worn ones), `count_of(world, holder, base) -> u32` (bag and worn), `move_item(world, holder, from, to) -> Result<(), Refusal>` over `Place::Cell(i)` and `Place::Worn(slot)`, `use_item(world, holder, cell) -> bool`, `use_item_on(world, holder, cell, target) -> bool` (PS-13), and `inventory(world, holder)`, a read of cells and slots. `move_item`: cell to cell merges a stack up to its `stack`, else swaps; cell to slot wears it when the base's `slot` is the slot's kind and its `requires` holds, a worn item there swapping back; slot to cell takes it off into an empty cell, or swaps with an item that fits the slot; slot to slot swaps within one kind. `Refusal` (`#[non_exhaustive]`): `NoItem`, `WrongSlot`, `Requires`, `Full`, `Stacked`. `use_item` spends one first when `consume`, so the list cannot reach the unit it spends, then runs the base's `use` through `run_list` (subject the holder, no other, at its centre). `use_item_on` takes the same path aimed at another character (the [simulation design](sim-design.md)'s PS-13, §16 and §17): the target as the list's subject, at the target's centre, with no other, as `use_item`'s list has none (the load checks a `use` list with no other), so a potion used on an ally acts on the ally; a target not alive is refused before the spend (false, nothing spent). 8.9's first part adds a site (PS-6).
- **The equipment status:** wearing applies, at the call, one infinite effect instance for the item, through `EffectRequest::apply_apart`: an inline `Effect` of the item's global stat lines, its resist lines and its base's `grant`, never merged with another instance, so two equal rings keep two; its serial sits in the worn slot. The item's `added` and `pen` lines go on the holder's `Abilities` under `item:<uid>` (none without `Abilities`, traced). Taking it off ends that instance by serial (`end_effect_serial`) and removes the source's lines. A status's lines reach `Stats` at once and its reads at the next `stats_apply`, as any effect's; `EffectApplied` and `EffectExpired` go out as today. `EffectInstance` keeps its 168 bytes: the apart path is a resolve flag the queued path never sets.
- **After a restore,** a named hook (`save::on_restore`, `items`) rebuilds every worn item's status from the data of the day, quietly: through crate-private forms of the end and the apart apply that send no `EffectApplied` or `EffectExpired` and trace nothing, so no cue replays at a load (the reactor reads both events, `react/cues.rs:5348`); it ends the saved instance by serial and removes the `item:<uid>` lines, then wears the item again as above, so what a worn item applies is what its tooltip shows (Q-i); a worn item whose base is unknown stays worn and applies nothing (A29, §2.3).
- **The `item` condition:** `{ "item": { "id": "net", "min": 3, "max": 9, "target": "other" } }`, holding when `count_of` lies in its inclusive bounds; `game` refused; the base checked at load. Boxed if it would grow `Condition`.
- **`give` and `take`:** `{ "give": { "item": "oil_flask", "count": 2, "target": "other" } }` (count 1–9,999, default 1; `self` by default, `game` refused), one boxed `item_action` payload each, so `CueAction` keeps its size; allowed in story lists and cues, refused in world cues (as `D-315`'s three).
- `ItemsPlugin` registers `Inventory` and `ItemLedger` saved, the component's description and the shapes; no system in this step.

### 2.5 Ground items and their labels (Step 3)

- **`ground_item`,** a component: `{ "item": "oil_flask" | { item }, "count": 2, "label": true, "offset": [0, -12] }`, `count` only beside a base ID; saved with the item whole.
- **Collection rides `pickup`:** a pickup whose entity holds a `ground_item` goes only to a collector whose `inventory` takes the item whole and that is not its dropper within its first second, and `Collector::run` puts the item in that bag at once, before the next pickup's check reads it, so two items never claim one cell (A30); `PickedUp` and the end follow as today. The gate and the give read `GroundItem` once a pickup, and only when `has_any::<GroundItem>()` (A5), so a World without one runs as before. The magnet still pulls toward a full bag (Q-o).
- **`drop_item(world, holder, cell, count) -> Option<Entity>`** spawns the base's `ground` prefab at the holder's centre (`centre_of`) holding the item, its dropper refused for 1 s of game time (`ground_item_tick` in `Phase::Tick`); a base without `ground` drops nothing. The load holds a `ground` prefab to carrying `ground_item` and `pickup`.
- **Labels:** `ground_item_labels`, in `Phase::Present`, pushes one world label a ground item with `label`: its built name (and `×n` past 1) at its centre plus `offset`, in the theme's text style `rarity.<rarity>` (its family as the font, its size, colour, outline and shadow, A9), else the theme's base text with a one-unit dark outline; core culls and caps (A9). The same style names the item in the screens (Step 7's `bind.style`, Q-d).

### 2.6 Shops (Step 4)

- **`shops`,** each entry: `name`; `currency` (a counter name, default `coins`); `pays` (`game`, the default, or `other`: whose counters hold it); `stock` (at most 256 `shop_entry`s, `{ item, count, price, when }`: a base listed once, `count` none for unlimited, `price` the base's by default, `when` a condition with the keeper as `self` and the customer as `other`); `buys` (a tag filter over a base's tags; none: buys nothing); `sell` (the share of the price paid, 0–1, default 0.5); `scale` (`price_scale`: `{ counter, target, per, min, max }`, the factor `clamp(1 + per × count, min, max)`, `min` and `max` in (0, 10], the count read from the keeper (`self`), the customer or the game). A buy costs `max(1, round(p × factor))`, `p` the entry's price (0: free); a sale pays `floor(q × sell)`, `q` what the shop would charge for that base now (its entry's price, else the base's, scaled the same way), so `sell` ≤ 1 keeps every sale at or under the buy and no buy-and-sell loop gains coin while the factor holds (Q-n).
- **A visit,** `ShopVisit`, one at a time, unsaved: `open_shop(world, id, keeper, customer) -> bool`, `visit(world)`, `price_of(world, base) -> Option<u32>`, `buy(world, base) -> Result<(), ShopRefusal>`, `sell(world, holder_cell, count) -> Result<u32, ShopRefusal>`, `end_shop(world)`. `ShopRefusal`: `Hidden` (its `when` fails), `SoldOut`, `Poor`, `Full`, `NotBought`. A buy checks everything first, then pays (a counter add of minus the price), gives and counts it in `ItemLedger.sold`; stock left is `count − sold`. Plain events `ShopOpened` and `ShopClosed`; a restore hook (`save::on_restore`) ends a visit.
- **`shop`,** a cue action: `{ "shop": "chandlery" }` opens a visit, the subject the keeper and the other the customer; allowed in story lists (a choice's `do`), refused in world cues. A visit a talk opens waits for the talk to end: the screen opens only while no talk runs (`Dialogue::current` none) and the screens are idle, since idle alone holds no talk back (A28).
- **"From day 4"** is a `when` on a game counter today; the third plan's clock may set that counter (Q-j).
- `ShopsPlugin` registers the kind, its check (conditions, the counter names valid, the tag filter declared, the bases known) and the restore hook.

### 2.7 The grid layout, and the keybindings on it (Step 5)

- **On the flex solver, not Taffy's grid** (Q-p): Taffy's `grid` feature stays off, since it grows every node's `Style` and so every relayout's allocation (A14). A grid is laid out by flex nodes core adds to that root's Taffy tree alone, so a root with no grid builds the tree it builds today, and every allocation pin over one holds. `D-124` is amended in `D-31[6]`: a grid without the feature.
- **Core:** `ui::Grid { columns: Vec<Track>, rows: Vec<Track> }` and `ui::Track` (`#[non_exhaustive]`: `Auto`, `Px(f32)`, `Fr(f32)`, finite, `Fr` above 0, columns only); `UiTree::set_grid(id, Option<Grid>) -> bool` and `grid(id)`; `widgets::grid(tree, parent, columns)`. The grid is held boxed in the node's control slot (`Control::Grid(Box<Grid>)`), so `Node` and `UiTree` keep their sizes (A15); a node holding a toggle, slider or field takes no grid (`set_grid` → false).
- **The layout:** `build` gives a grid node a flex column (`gap` between rows) of synthetic flex rows (`gap` between cells), its flowed children `columns.len()` a row in order; an anchored child stays the grid node's own, a collapsed one takes no cell. A cell's width is its column's: `Px` fixed; `Fr(n)` grows by `n` from a basis of 0; `Auto` the widest max-content width among that column's cells, measured by solving each at max-content in the same tree before the root's solve, then fixed. Its height is its own `height` in the row (`Fill` stretches to the row's height), a `Px` row fixing the synthetic row's. `place` adds a synthetic row's location to its cells', so hit testing, paint and focus see only the tree's own nodes; the synthetic nodes are never in `map` or `dump_layout()`.
- **Screen files:** `layout.columns` (1–32 tracks: `"auto"`, a length (units, a size name, `spacing.*`) or `"<n>fr"`) and `layout.rows` (`"auto"` or a length), on every container kind and `repeat`; `sizes()` and the schema list them (A17).
- **The keybindings (S-162):** one grid under the card, columns `["auto", "1fr", "1fr", "1fr", "auto"]`: the header's five cells (an empty caption, the three column notes, an empty cell where the hidden Reset was), then each action's caption, its three cells and its Reset; `row()` and `CAPTION` go, every key and name stays (A16). 01's copy follows byte for byte.

### 2.8 Drag and drop between widgets (Step 6)

- **The role:** `Node`'s `drag_handle` bool becomes one byte of flags (handle, source, target), so `Node` keeps its size; `set_drag_handle` keeps its meaning beside `set_draggable` and `set_drop_target` and their reads. A widget is a handle or a source, not both: the later call wins.
- **Routing:** a primary press on a source is captured as today; past `drag_threshold` it starts an item drag, `UiEvent::DragStarted { widget, key }`. While it lasts, hover follows the front-most visible, enabled drop target under the pointer, the source apart, inside the shown modal root when there is one, so its `hovered` style marks it. A release over a target sends `UiEvent::Dropped { widget, key, target }`, the target's key read from the tree (`UiTree::key`) so `UiEvent` keeps its 40 bytes (Step 6's run), anything else `UiEvent::DragCancelled { widget, key }`; `ui_cancel`, the pointer leaving and the source removed, hidden or disabled cancel, and that `ui_cancel` reaches no screen. A drag never activates; a release before the threshold activates as today. `UiRouter::dragging()` reads the source.
- **The ghost:** at the start the router builds a root on `GamePopup`, bare, anchored at its top left and not hit-testable, holding a copy of the source's first image (sprite, size, tint) at 75 % opacity, and moves it with the pointer at the grab's offset; it goes with the drag. A source with no image shows none. The router is not boxed again each frame (A15), so its new state moves no guard.
- **Screen files:** `drag` and `drop`, two booleans every kind takes, set at build, rows included.
- **Harness:** `UiHarness::drag(from, to)`: the pointer to `from`'s centre, down, a step, past the threshold, a step, to `to`'s centre, a step, up.

### 2.9 Images, tooltips and popups in screen files (Step 7)

- **`image`,** a kind: `sprite` (an ID), or `field` (a row's field naming one), or `bind.image` (a binding naming one); `tint` (`#rrggbb` or `#rrggbbaa`); its layout's size, else the sprite's own. An empty or unknown name collapses the node, warned once a name. `UiBindings::image(name, TextBinding)`. `tungsten check` holds a literal `sprite` to the manifest's sprites.
- **Styles by data:** `bind.style` (a binding naming a theme text style, applied compare-on-write; empty: none) on the text kinds, and `style_field`, a row's field naming one, inside a repeat.
- **`tooltip`,** a field every kind takes: a string, or `{ "screen": "<id>" }`. The build records each node's tooltip in its screen's live part, and `screen_build::tooltip(world, widget)` reads it.
- **`popup`,** an action beside `pop`, `push`, `action` and `setting`: `{ "popup": "<id>" }` opens a screen beside the control.
- **Tooltips** (`screens::popup`): `popup::tick(world, root)`, called once a frame by `ModalScreen::update`, by `DataScreen::update` and by the dialogue box's update, which calls neither while its talk runs (A24), and by Step 8's screens, so every `tungsten::screens` modal screen shows them and no system runs while none is open. A widget of the screen hovered, or holding visible focus, for `TooltipSettings::delay` (0.5 s, real clock) shows its tooltip: a string as a `small` label in a panel at most 320 units wide; a screen file as a non-modal `game_popup` root, built when shown, refreshed each frame and removed when hidden. It sits beside the widget, below it, above when the viewport has no room below, shifted inside the viewport by its last laid-out size, hidden until its first layout; it takes no hits. Leaving, a press, a drag, a scroll, or the widget hidden or removed hides it. `tooltip_anchor(world)` names the widget and its key for a tooltip screen's bindings. `set_tooltip(world, widget, Tooltip)` gives a Rust-built widget one. A tooltip's root, and a popup's, is owned by the state whose screen showed it (`StateStack::own_root`), so it hides while that state is covered and goes when it exits, though no tick follows the last screen's exit (the umbrella's `state.rs:248-259`, `:487-490`).
- **Popups:** `screens::popup::open(world, id, beside) -> bool` pushes a screen file whose root is a modal `game_popup` root as a `ModalScreen` state, placed as a tooltip is; a press outside its root or Cancel pops it; its actions run as a data screen's; `popup_anchor(world)` names the control it opened beside and its key for its bindings and actions.
