## 2. Design

### 2.1 The frame graph (Step 1)

A module `graph`, private to the render crate. A frame is declared, compiled and run; the declaration is the only place that knows the frame's shape.

- **Textures.** A declaration names textures: one imported, the frame's output (the surface's texture, or a direct capture's stand-in), and transients described by a size rule from the output (`Output`, or the output shifted right for bloom's levels), a format rule, a sample count and a mip count. A use names a texture and a mip.
- **Passes** are declared in draw order, each with its kind (what records it), its wgpu label, its timing label, what it reads, and what it writes and how: `Clear(colour)` (drawn over a clear: the pass leaves pixels unwritten), `Whole` (every pixel written with blending off) or `Over` (drawn over what is there). The scene pass adds its resolve target and its depth attachment.
- **The output is derived.** The declaration chains the full-screen stages: the scene writes the first version of the frame's colour, each stage reads a version and writes the next, the overlay draws `Over` the last, and `present(last)` ends the frame. Compiling substitutes the imported output for that last version when no pass samples it and nothing copies from it: the stage that wrote it is `D-087`'s swapchain writer, whichever stage it is. A capture marks the last version as copied from, so it stays a transient and a blit pass to the output follows: the capture frame is the same declaration with one mark. A direct capture imports the stand-in as the output and blits it to the surface in a last pass.
- **Compile** (pure, tested without a GPU): drop the passes nothing reads (a pass is live when it writes an imported texture or a copied-from one, or a live pass reads what it writes); find each texture's first and last use; choose the ops; give each transient a pooled texture, two transients sharing one when their uses do not overlap and their descriptors are equal; and set the acquire split at the first pass that writes the imported output (`D-145`).
- **Ops from first and last use.** A first use loads by its write: `Clear` clears; `Whole` loads an offscreen target (today's tuned op, A5) and clears the imported output (until Step 2); `Over` on a first use is a declaration error. A later use loads. A store is `Store` while a later pass reads the texture, or it is the output or copied from; otherwise `Discard`, which today's frame gives the depth target and the multisampled colour (its resolve target is written whatever the store, A8's source).
- **The pool** holds textures by descriptor (size, format, samples, mips, usage, the SMAA twin's view format), each with an identity that never repeats. A compiled frame is kept while its shape (the declaration's inputs) holds, so its textures and every bind group over them are stable from frame to frame; the caches of A4 key on texture identities in place of the pool's generation. A texture no frame has used for 240 frames is freed, and every texture of another size is freed at a resize. The late acquire's re-record keys on the output's size and a count of resizes.
- **What changes in the picture: nothing.** What changes beside it: the depth target and the multisampled colour store `Discard`; a frame holds only the targets it uses (example 01 at 2160p: the scene colour, the pyramid and the depth target; no `post_ping`, `post_pong`; under `cpu_stable` no depth target, closing known issues' render F11's depth half); a stack of two or more passes alternates between two full-size targets, the scene colour's among them, where it held three; a stack that ends in bloom records its pyramid passes before the acquire, the split falling on the composite (A6); a capture frame's blit clears the output itself.
- **Timing labels** are generated at compile under today's names (A7) and kept with the compiled frame, so a timed frame formats none; the ring's layout is the compiled frame's pass count, one more for a capture's blit, and the size.
- **Bloom, `D-301` and `D-302`** become declarations: the pyramid is a transient with its levels, sized by `render.bloom_base` from the output; the threshold, the downsamples, the upsamples and the composite are passes; the fold is a rule of the declaration until Step 3 replaces it.
- **Public surface.** `tungsten_render::passes`, `RenderTargetPool`, `SceneTarget`, `PostStackRenderer::plan_targets` and `record_pass`, and SMAA's three unused `*_pass_desc` builders leave the crate's public API; nothing outside the crate uses them (the frame-surface audit, (f)).

### 2.2 The swapchain's clear (Step 2)

- **Whose pass writes every pixel.** The graph takes a pass for `Whole` only when it can know it: the pass's kind is one of the engine's full-screen kinds (a stock or composed post pass, bloom's threshold, downsamples and composite, SMAA's three, the present blit), whose pipelines draw one triangle over the whole target with no blend and every channel written, and its shader text is the engine's own, compiled-in or generated. The renderer keeps, for each engine shader, whether the text in use is the compiled-in one; a load or a reload that changes that is part of the frame's shape, so the declaration compiles again, and a file restored to the compiled-in bytes is the engine's own again. A pass drawn by a replaced file, or by a game's own effect, is declared `Clear`: a replacement may discard.
- **`DontCare`.** A `Whole` first write of the imported output loads with `LoadOp::DontCare`. The token comes from one `unsafe` call in one function, the crate's only `unsafe`. On DX12 and GL the op is a load of a texture wgpu-core counts as written, so every backend draws the same picture.
- **What else the rule moves.** SMAA's three passes, each of which draws every pixel with the compiled-in shaders, are `Whole` from this step: its edges and weights targets load where they cleared. And an offscreen stock or bloom pass drawn by a replaced file clears its target, where since `a2999d3e` it loaded whatever its shader did.

### 2.3 The post composer (Step 3)

- **The contract.** A stock post file stays a whole module and gains a marked section, between `// @fx begin <class>` and `// @fx end`, holding one function and its helpers, every name prefixed with the effect's: `fn <name>_fx(color: vec4<f32>, uv: vec2<f32>, p: Params) -> vec4<f32>` for an effect that reads its own pixel, or `fn <name>_head(uv: vec2<f32>, p: Params) -> vec4<f32>` for one that samples `src` itself. The file's own `fs_main` calls that function, so a pass drawn alone runs the same arithmetic as today. A section that reads its own pixel is checked with Naga when it is read: its function and the helpers it calls name no module-scope variable (no `src`, no `params`), so a fused stage cannot sample around the colour it is handed. A section that fails is no section, with the reason logged, and its file draws alone.
- **Classes.**
  - `head` samples `src` itself: chromatic_aberration, crt, glitch, pixelate, god_rays, pixel_outline, and bloom's composite, whose module also binds the pyramid.
  - `mix`: each output channel is its own input channel times a factor from 0 to 1, plus a constant from 0 up; the factor and the constant may follow the pixel's position and the parameters, never the colour. Vignette, fade, fog, wipe_radial and dissolve, while the pass's colour parameter is not negative, which the declaration checks.
  - `pixel` reads its own pixel and is continuous in it: tonemap, color_adjust, film_grain, lut, tone_mono.
  - `steps` reads its own pixel and quantises or branches on it: dither.
- **Under 8-bit targets one case fuses: the stack's last two stages, when the last is a `mix`** and the one before it has a valid section of any class. Every stage before them is drawn alone, as today. The generated pass is the shared prefix, a two-block uniform array, the two sections and an `fs_main` that calls the first, clamps colour and alpha to 0..1 as the 8-bit target between the two passes did, and calls the second.
- **Why only that.** The bound is on the stack's output, so it has to survive every pass after a fused one: a later pass that amplifies or quantises (dither's `floor`, film grain's subtraction near black, color_adjust's gain) turns a one-step difference into several, which is why `D-302` folded only a last pass; so nothing follows a fused pass. And it has to hold in the encoded space the target rounds in. The fused pass loses one rounding, of the first stage's output: at most 0.6 of a step, the backends' conversion tolerance. A `mix` does not grow it there: for `w·x + c` with `w` in 0..1 and `c` not negative the slope in sRGB-encoded values is at most 1.02, the excess at the curve's junction. The two pipelines then differ by at most 0.62 of a step before the last rounding and by at most one after it. Nothing wider keeps that: an added signed constant takes a value near 0.08 to the curve's foot and half a step to 3 (film grain); a luma mix reaches a slope of 1.3 near the foot, and a duotone whose tints fall as luma rises turns white's half step into three (lut, tone_mono); a second lost rounding could pass one step, so a fused pass holds two stages.
- **Under float targets** (`render.hdr`, §2.5) a pass rounds to its target's float format, not to 8 bits, and the picture has no frame of today's to keep: every stage from the stack's last `steps` stage on fuses, a `head` starting a new pass, eight stages a pass at most, no clamp between stages; the stages before that `steps` stage draw alone, so a quantising stage reads what it would read unfused. Under `rgba16f` a lost rounding is at most 2⁻¹¹ of a value, and Step 5 holds the fused frame within one 8-bit step of the stages drawn alone on the fixtures' stacks. Under `rg11b10` a stage drawn alone rounds to 6- and 5-bit mantissas at every pass, about two 8-bit steps in bright blue, which a fused pass does not: there the fused frame is the more exact, and the difference is a stated measurement on Step 5's sheet, not a bound.
- **A stage alone** is drawn by its own pipeline; a file without a valid section, replaced or a game's, always is.
- **The owner's "URP UberPost's order"** is read as the shape of a generated pass (a head that moves or gathers pixels, bloom's add, then the per-pixel chain, the output's conversion last), not as a reordering of the game's stack: an order other than the stack's would break the one-step bound (Q2). The owner's "runs of per-pixel effects" hold under float targets; under 8-bit targets the bound leaves the last pair. Both readings are told to the owner at the close.
- **`D-302`** is then the pair `[bloom's composite, vignette]`, and its conditions widen: a `mix` that ends the stack fuses behind any section (`[vignette, fade]` under a screen transition, `[pixelate, fade]` at game over), a frame with an SMAA tail by Q4. `fs_vignette`, `fold_vignette`, `trailing_vignette` and the two replaced flags are removed, §2.2's record of replaced shaders standing in for the flags.
- **Cache and reload.** Generated modules and their pipelines are cached by the pass's signature (its stages' kinds, the target format) and built at a signature's first use through `build_validated`; a failed build draws the stages alone and is not retried until a reload. A shader reload re-reads that effect's section and drops every cached pass that holds it.
- **A switch.** `render.post_fuse` (`true`; `TUNGSTEN_RENDER_POST_FUSE`), read at start-up: `false` draws every stage alone. The pixel tests compare the two.
- **Labels.** A fused pass is timed as `post<N>_` and its stages' names joined by `_`, `N` its first slot: `post0_bloom_composite_vignette` keeps its name.

### 2.4 Game-authored post passes (Step 4)

- **Manifest.** A `post_effects` section mirrors `materials`: `"<id>": { "shader": "<shaders id>", "uniform_defaults": { … } }`, the shader a whole module with the stock prefix (`docs/assets.md` gives it), optionally with an `@fx` section of one of §2.3's classes. Core gains `PostEffectId` and `PostEffectRegistry` beside the material registry (no wgpu type).
- **The mark is the author's.** `head` and `steps` promise nothing: such an effect only ever leads a fused pass, reading stored pixels. `mix` and `pixel` are promises the engine cannot check (§2.3's definitions): a wrong one can move that effect's own fused output by more than a step, and the docs say so. What the engine does check is the section's shape and that a per-pixel section names no module-scope variable. An effect without a valid section draws alone.
- **The stack.** `PostPass` gains a nineteenth variant, `Custom(CustomPost)`: the effect's ID and the block's public lanes (`vec4`, `f32s`, `i32s`), `Copy`. A pass is built by the registry, `PostEffectRegistry::pass(name) -> Option<CustomPost>`, with the manifest's defaults in its lanes, and changed by setters; a pass a game holds keeps its lanes over a manifest reload, which changes only what the registry builds next. Its serde form is skipped: a room file cannot name one in this pass (Q7). `kind_name` returns `custom`.
- **Render.** `Renderer::upload_post_effect` builds the effect's own pipeline through `build_validated` and reads its section; an effect whose build failed is left out of the frame's declaration, its slot drawing nothing, and is logged once.
- **The decision** amends `D-058`'s closed set and `D-054`'s reading of it: the stock set stays closed and a game's effect is a manifest entry, not a runtime registration by code.

### 2.5 The HDR scene (Step 5)

- **The key.** `render.hdr`: `off` (the default), `rg11b10` or `rgba16f`; `TUNGSTEN_RENDER_HDR`; read at start-up like `msaa`. `rg11b10` asks for `Rg11b10Ufloat` and falls back to `Rgba16Float`, with a warning, where the device does not render and blend it (`device_format_flags`, the MSAA fallback's pattern); `rgba16f` keeps the scene's alpha, which `pixel_outline` reads, at about 1.1 ms more a frame at 2160p (the probes).
- **Formats.** Under HDR the scene colour, its multisampled twin and every version of the frame's colour before the last are float; the scene pass's pipelines are built for that format; the last full-screen pass writes the 8-bit output through a tail the composer appends to it: the clamp to 0..1, which is today's look, then a dither of one 8-bit step against banding. An opt-in operator is the stack's own `Tonemap` pass. A stack whose last pass cannot take the tail (a replaced file, a game's own module), and an empty stack, get a pass of the tail alone. SMAA and the overlay then read and draw on 8-bit targets as today, so the UI is drawn after the tonemap; a capture reads the 8-bit frame.
- **Fusing under HDR** is §2.3's float rule: the stages from the last `steps` stage on, eight a pass, the tail on the last pass.
- **Bloom** reads values above 1 where the scene holds them; its threshold and knee are the game's.
- **Off** draws the base's picture byte for byte: one format value at every site of A14, no added pass.

### 2.6 Tiled lights (Step 6)

- **Binning.** A compute pass, declared before the scene pass, writes for each screen tile of 32 pixels one bit a packed light: set for every directional light, and for each point light whose radius, with the slack the vertex mark uses, reaches the tile's box in world space, taken from the camera's inverse. A tile holds `ceil(cap / 32)` words, so no light is ever left out for room and nothing has to be ranked: 33 KB at 2160p at a cap of 16, 261 KB at 256. One invocation writes one tile's words, so the result does not depend on scheduling.
- **The graph's buffers.** The declaration gains named buffers beside its textures: a pass lists the buffers it writes and reads, and liveness follows them as it follows textures. The compute pass writes the tile buffer; the scene pass reads it when the frame draws a lit batch; a frame without one drops the compute pass.
- **The lit shader** finds its tile from the fragment's position and walks the tile's set bits from the lowest, which is packed order, each term as today. A light whose bit is clear adds exactly zero at every fragment of the tile (A16), so at a cap of 16 the picture is the base's byte for byte, which the oracle holds.
- **Bindings.** Group 2 keeps binding 0, `LightUbo`'s 544 bytes with the first 16 packed lights (its three spare counts carry the tile grid), and adds the lights past 16 and the tile words as read-only storage buffers; a game's own lit file written against binding 0 alone still draws the first 16.
- **The cap.** `render.light_cap`, 16 by default and at most 256, read at start-up; `LIGHT_CAP` stays the default's name; `cull_to_cap` and `extract_lights` take the cap. The `gpu` row and example 01 draw at 16.
- **The mark.** The vertex stage's `u32` mark covers 32 lights. The step measures the scene pass with tiles alone and with tiles beside a mark held to the first 32, and keeps the lower (Q9).
- **No second path.** Compute and read-only storage in the fragment stage are within the default limits the device is created with (A18), so no device the renderer creates lacks them.

### 2.7 Checks, the oracle and the timed batch

- **The gate `G`** of a code step, untimed, each exit 0: `cargo fmt --all -- --check`; `cargo test -p tungsten-render --locked`; `just check`; `just repo-check`; `just ctx`; `just api` then `just api-check` where the public surface moves; `just smoke`; `just visual` (six `test result: ok.` lines) with `git status --short` over the three fixture folders printing only what the step names; `WGPU_BACKEND=vulkan just perf counters` at the pins the step states.
- **The pixel oracle** (machine-local `perf-runs/20261123-gpu-foundation/tools/oracle.py`): `capture <label>` stores a set of captures from a set of binaries, the base's from the base's kept binaries run in the base's export, a step's from the tree's as the step's gate ends; `compare` reads two stored sets byte for byte. The set: example 01's nine stills (the six areas at 2160p, `e01-pit`, `e01-stress`, `e01-play`); the bench's `visual` preset under eight knob sets (post `light`, `none` and `full`; `aa` off, MSAA 4 and SMAA) on the capture path and the direct one, three of them under `gpu_depth`; the fixtures' post stacks (`empty`, `all`, `retro_arcade`, `dreamy`, `glitch_boss`, `bloom_only`, `fade_pair`, `fade_twice`), UI lab, lighting, seams and transition scenes. `oracle.py compare <label> <label>` prints `N of N equal` and exits 0 when every pair is; where a step allows a bound it counts decoded colour and alpha apart (pixels that differ, pixels past one step, the largest difference of each). A bound has two rules, never mixed: **the stack's output**, captured with the overlay off and no SMAA tail, within one step in every channel, a hard gate; and **the frame**, under the overlay's blend or SMAA's edge test, which can grow a one-step difference (`D-302` read three steps in 8 pixels of two million under a HUD): under the overlay at most three steps, in at most 0.01 % of the frame's pixels, and one step elsewhere; under SMAA by Q4. A capture outside its rule stops the step. This is the owner's "stills byte-equal, `just visual` at tolerance 0": `just visual`'s two golden tests compare at 2 against fixtures older than `D-302`, so the tree is held to the base's own captures instead.
- **Timed checks are batched** for when the owner's NoMachine client is off (`pgrep -x nxcodec.bin` prints nothing), each step committed on `G` and its oracle with its timed lines marked "not run yet" in the evidence log. A step's sitting is `WGPU_BACKEND=vulkan just perf sitting --base b12958b4 --rows gpu,gpu-throughput,template,e01-play,e01-areas,boot --wait-quiet 900 --keep` → every row `ok`, then `judged.py` → every judged pair's digests match (corrected at Step 4's sitting: every pair's of a row with a `physics:` line; `boot`'s digest is its `bench:` counters', and its `units` reads 2,883 against the base's 2,882 from Step 4's load unit on, so `judged.py` exits 1 on that row alone). A sitting names the commit it judges: taken while the tree is the step's commit it is the command above, its `cand.commit` quoted; taken after a later commit it sits an export of the step's own commit (machine-local `sit_export.py <export> <commit> -- --base b12958b4 --rows …`), never the later tree. A step's binaries are kept at its gate (`builds.py keep step<N>`), and by-hand readings (`probe_run.py areas` and `bench`, two runs a binary, each from its own tree) run those. A step's own move is its sitting's delta less the step before's (`step_delta.py`). Step 7 sits every row.
- **Subagents** verify, never write the tree: a fresh review of each code step's diff before its commit, and the oracle's and the sittings' readers. Code steps run serially in the main tree.
