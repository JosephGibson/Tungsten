## 2. Design

### 2.1 Scope, order and the tests

Five rows, seven steps and the close. Step 1 goes first, so every later tree despawn is the kit's. Step 2 goes before Step 3: both edit `title.rs`, on lines that do not meet. Steps 5 and 6 each append a name to the tags file, in that order. Step 7 touches the template alone. No step needs another to have landed (§6).

A test's asserts stand. A step may port a test's reads or its setup to the new owner (the clock stepped where it was set, a tag for a marker, a filter where a missile's every child was a drip, a glow hidden where its `Glow` was removed), and changes an asserted value only where its Moves name it. New tests are the done-when's alone (the memory's rule for examples): one each at Steps 1, 3 and 4, for a behaviour no test holds today.

### 2.2 `despawn_tree` on the kit's recursive despawn (Step 1, S-177)

`shared::despawn_tree` keeps its name, its signature and its 30 call sites. Its body becomes a fresh `CommandBuffer`, the kit's `despawn_recursive` queued on it and `World::flush` of that buffer, as the kit's own example flushes one (`K/transform/hierarchy.rs:233-237`). The world's own buffer, with what the step has queued, is not touched (A2).

- **Moves:** none in what dies or when (A2). The kit also drops the root from its parent's `Children` in the call, where 01's walk left the entry for the next propagation to prune. A call allocates twice, the buffer's command list and the kit's stack, where the hand walk allocated once; none lands in the pit's window (A14), so the pin holds.
- A new test holds the row's point, that the children go in the call itself: a black hole's dust is dead when `despawn_tree` returns, before any step. The suite's other tests cannot tell, since a frame's first propagation pass also takes a dead parent's children.

### 2.3 The level's clock on `Time` (Step 2, S-136)

- `drawn_time` reads the engine's clock: `Time::elapsed()` less one fixed step while the clock interpolates, less `alpha × step` otherwise, in `f64`, floored at 0 and returned as `f32`; 0 with no `Time`. By A4 that is today's value plus the game seconds before the run's spawn.
- `SceneTime`, `systems::clock` and the `level_clock` registration go, with the resource's insert and removal. The two "a run is live" reads take `AreaLight`, which `level::spawn` and `level::despawn` insert and remove beside the clock (`mod.rs:270`, `:338`). The docs A13 names lose the clock.
- The two tests that set the clock step it, with `Time::advance_frame` on the World's clock and no stage run: a whole `EMITTER_DWELL` in `burning`'s, which moves the hop by one whatever the base, and 0.25 s between `presentation`'s two draws.
- **Moves:** the drawn clock no longer starts at 0 with the run. It counts the game seconds since the app started, the title's among them, so each pulse, drift, flicker and hop keeps its rate and takes a phase that differs from run to run, where every run began alike; a load no longer rewinds it. A frame that drops steps, under 30 fps, moves it by the frame's whole game dt, where the summed steps fell behind. Nothing but presentation reads it (A3), and the pit holds (A14).
- Beside the tests, a scratch test proves the zero point is all that moved (Step 2's done-when).

### 2.4 The title's shooting star on the kit (Step 3, S-169)

- The star's emitter spawns, in one bundle, with what `emitter` gives it today and `LinearMotion::new(METEOR_HEADING.normalize() * METEOR_SPEED)`, `Position(start)`, `PrevPosition(start)` and `Lifetime::new(METEOR_SECS)`. `TitleSky::meteor` holds the entity alone and clears once it is dead. The by-hand move and despawn go. The title's own clock and its count of stars, the cadence, stay (A5).
- The four star constants become `pub`, so the new test reads them.
- **Moves,** measured (A14): the star flies on the game clock in fixed steps, drawn between its last two. It rests at its start for its spawn frame and one more at 60 Hz, since `title_sky` spawns it after the frame's steps, then crosses on the same line at the same speed; it lives 0.7 s of game time and leaves at that frame's flush, 1,000 px out where the hand code reached 1,025 or 1,050 before the first frame past 0.7 s. Under frames slower than 30 fps the engine drops steps, and the star, moved and aged by them, crosses in up to twice the real time (21 frames and 1.4 s at 15 fps, 1.05 s at 20), while its particles, ticked by the frame's whole game dt, age at their real pace, so its trail is as much shorter; the hand code flew by real dt at any rate (Q11). Under the level editor's stop it holds with its particles. Under a screen pushed over the title it flies on, as on the real clock.
- `docs/known-issues.md`'s P3 row cites the star's line in `title.rs`: the step moves the citation to the spawn condition's line and leaves the row's text.

### 2.5 The glows' parts as their owners' children (Step 4, S-128)

- `spawn_parts` spawns each part with the kit's `spawn_child`: the light at `glow.offset`, the halo and the core at their corners' offsets, each with its `SceneEntity` as today. `GlowPart` goes, and with it the sweep for parts whose owner has gone: a tree despawn takes them with their owner, and the kit's first pass takes a dead parent's (A7).
- `GlowParts` stays the owner's record of its parts, which the tests read. The prefab's `glow` gives it with the `Glow`, unspawned, in the spawn's own move (`register_component_with`), and `spawn_parts` fills it in place; a `Glow` built in code, as one test builds two, takes it by an insert as today. An owner's one move is then the kit's `Children`, where inserting `GlowParts` after the spawns cost a second and 2.0 MB in the pit (A14).
- Each frame `glows` sets each part's `LocalOffset`, its point less the owner's drawn point, and, as today, its `Transform` (point and scale), its visibility and the light's intensity. The kit's passes hold a part to its owner through the frame. `glows` sets the point for its own frame because it runs after both passes, and must: the player's lantern offset is read off the frame the animator shows, in the present phase, and a halo's corner moves with its pulse (A6, A7). So no part is drawn a frame behind its offset.
- The lantern's embers stay a map object the generator sets on its lantern (A11, §2.10).
- **Moves:** a part leaves with its owner in the owner's own despawn: at once under `despawn_tree`, at the flush under the kit's despawn, which first marks it `Despawning`; today it stayed until the next `glows` run. A live owner that lost its `Glow` would now keep its parts; no source site removes one (A6), and the test that did hides the glows instead. Nothing drawn moves: a scratch test holds each part's point, scale, visibility and intensity to today's bits (Step 4's done-when). A glow owner's `Children` lists its parts, so `fixed_step`'s test takes the missile's children that hold a `ParticleEmitter` as its drips, and the census names `Children` rebuilt for the orb and the player. ### 2.7 The map's saved objects by a tag (Step 6, S-111)

- A name cannot class them (A10). So the class is a prefab's: `level.saved`, declared in the tags file and appended last to `tags` on every prefab that carries one of the six kinds: the eleven content prefabs (`bat`, `beetle`, `crawler`, `dummy`, `goblin`, `troll`, `troll_armoured`, `wisp`, `health_orb`, `mana_orb`, `geyser`) and, in `tools/level.py`, the basins', the fires' and spikes' and both platform branches' (no fire or spike is placed today). `level::saved` becomes one read: the entity's `Tags` hold `level.saved`. A kind a save should name is then data.
- `lava::start` moves ahead of the kept IDs in `level::spawn`. It reads `Hazard` alone and inserts `Lava`; a basin then never stands with the kept ID and no `Lava`, so its chain is an archetype shorter, which pays for its tag in every body query's list (A15).

### 2.9 The pit's protocol

01's pit pin is exact. Steps 4 and 6 move it; each does this in place of the plain guard check:

1. With the step built and every other test passing, `cargo test --test game -- guards::` passes, or fails on `allocations` or `allocated_bytes` alone.
2. The probe (`W/pit-probe/pit_probe.sh`: the guard's size histogram for the step's tree and for its base, then per-size backtraces for each size whose count differs; `diff.py` over the two) attributes every differing bucket to a site. The base is a tree that reads the pin as it stands: HEAD's copy of every file the step changed that the run loads, its `assets/` files among them (`BASE_FILES`). `U/testing/guard.rs`, which the probe patches, is restored from HEAD and compared (`cmp`) after each run. 3. The move is accepted when every bucket's site is in the step's allowance and each pinned line is within its bound; the evidence row quotes the old and new lines and the decomposition (size, count, site, cause).
4. Then `TUNGSTEN_GUARDS_WRITE=1 cargo test --test game -- guards::`, `git diff tests/game/guards/pit.json` showing those lines alone, and `cargo test --test game -- guards::` → exit 0.

Anything else stops the step (§6): another line moving, a site outside the allowance, a bucket no backtrace explains, or a line past its bound.
