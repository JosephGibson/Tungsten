- **The pit.** `level.json` gains `pit` `{left, right, rim, floor}`, the interior's columns and rows; the clearing's ground is cut from the row under `rim` down to `floor`, its faces in the pit-wall family, with a shell four tiles thick below and beside it. Interior at least 3.8 Mpx² (starting value 48 × 20 tiles, 3.9 Mpx², A18), so `BALL_CAP` small balls settle under the rim. A rope bridge at the rim runs from the pit's west edge to a 3-tile opening against its east wall, since a deck stops a rising player from below (A24): `main` crosses the bridge and jumps the opening to the east rim; `pit_in` drops from the bridge's east end through the opening to the floor, and `pit_climb` climbs shelves on the east wall from the floor to the east rim, so a fall in has a way out.
- **Walls and their layers (§2.4).** Iron cladding covers the mine's terrace faces, the gallery's ceiling and wall faces elsewhere in the mine, never a walking surface. A mine cell is iron when it has open air on its left, right or below, or stands one cell behind such a face, and has none above. Every other mine cell, the treads and the cut's inside, draws and collides as rock. Iron cells leave the tile collision layer for merged static boxes in physics layer 2 (`IRON_LAYER`). Wooden decks and scaffolds (`bridge_rope`, `bridge_weathered`) move to layer 3 (`WOOD_LAYER`); stone slabs stay in layer 1. Tiles stay layer 0.
- **Enemies.** 13 on the map (`MAP_ENEMIES`), none in the clearing; each crawler on a ledge 12 tiles or longer, each bat with a ceiling within 480 px above it, each wisp with 3 tiles of open air about its home, each beetle on an open-cut floor 16 tiles or longer under the sky, the troll on the bridge's flat (A14).
- **Hazards and movers.** Six spikes (the terrace's first, then the aqueduct, the mine and the bridge) and five fires, all in the hollow, each fire's swept box clear of terrain; the hollow's two lava basins (Q17); four moving platforms, the clearing's lift first.
- **Lava (Q17).** A lake in the hollow's pit, three tiles or more across, split by a basalt stepping stone that `main` crosses with one single jump a pair, and a pool one tile deep sunk in a shelf. Each basin is a hazard over its surface that hurts as the fires do. The lake carries a light in the sixth fire's place; the pool and the lavafall glow through their emissive yellows alone.
- **Lights.** No 92 × 52-tile window, the 16:9 view at the 0.35 minimum zoom, holds more than 10 of the map's lights (glowing props, fires, the lava lake, wisps' homes and the troll's fire), which leaves room under 16 for the moon, the player's lantern, a strike and three fire missiles at every zoom a 16:9 window allows (A5, A25, A27). More missiles in flight (six at most), flares, summoned wisps or a wider window zoomed out can still pass 16, and the renderer then keeps lights in query order, not by nearness, so the lantern can drop: a known issue step 6 records.
- **Emitters.** Summed `max_alive` 256 at most, the existing check (A6).
- **Routes.** `main` from the spawn to the bridge's east end along the lowest walkable path, through every transition, one single jump a pair; alternates so every platform lies on a route (today's `forest_shrine` kept, `pit_in` and `pit_climb`, the aqueduct's upper ledges, the ravine's two walls, the cut's upper terraces). Long flat runs (the pit's bridge, the cut's floors, the troll's bridge) are the bowling lanes; stepped drops join floors.
- **Loose iron.** Four class-less `iron` markers on the cut's terraces near the beetles, as lightning rods, off `main`'s walking line, since a resting block is a wall to the controller (A17, Q5).

### 2.3 The generator's rules

- `build_level` keeps backdrop strips out of the tileset catalog by a list of today's five (`tools/level.py:182`); the far strips, the mist bands and `sky_black` join it in step 2, before they exist as images, or they enter the catalog as 64-px tiles.
- A ground platform's `style` names its family: `ground` (masonry, today's), `wet` (the ravine's rock), `basalt`, `mine` (the cut's rock and its iron), `pit` (the pit's lining); decks keep their styles.
- `build_level` picks each occupied tile from its family as today, and also: a tile with open air below takes its family's underside; a tile whose left or right neighbour is of another family takes that pair's border; a tile in `pit`'s walls or floor takes the pit family. The wet and mine families pick each tile by its open sides, one of 16 masks, and a variant. A mine cell takes iron cladding or rock by §2.1's rule.
- Iron cells leave the collision layer; their runs merge into the fewest static boxes (greedy rectangles), each an object in a new `iron` layer after `slabs` with the class `ex10_iron_<w>x<h>` (a static body in `IRON_LAYER`); wooden decks' slab bodies take `WOOD_LAYER`, stone ones keep layer 1.
- An indoor area's empty tiles with terrain above them in their column take the cave backs in the background layer, mixed as §2.2 lists, each tunnel mouth once. `level.json` gains `backs`: spans `{family, left, right, top}` of background-layer rock in outdoor areas: the ravine's far wall (`wet_back`, streaming behind the falls) and the cut's far terraces (`cut_back`). Each span is filled from `top` down to the terrain, its tiles' edges by their open sides within the background layer.
- `level.json` gains `lava`: basins `{left, right, row}`. A basin's open cells from `row` down to the terrain draw lava props, the surface clip on its top row and the deep clip under it. Each basin is a hazard object `ex10_lava_<i>` in `hazards` after the fires: `hazard` `{fire: false, lava: true}` and `contact_damage` over its surface row, the lake's with a `glow` (its light).
- `landmarks` gain `indoor` (default false), `ambience` (a sound ID), `backdrop` (a far strip's sprite ID, outdoor areas only) and `night` (default false: the area's sky is `sky_black`, §2.4); each area's prefab writes them into its `area`. Scenery goes in `props` as today's does.
- `iron` markers go to the `markers` layer, class-less; `dummies` then `enemies` stay the map's last layers.
- `validate_level` adds:
  - the landmarks' columns run contiguous from 0 to `cols - 1`;
  - the tests' sky holds nothing (§2.1);
  - no 92 × 52 window holds more than 10 lights;
  - each bat has a ceiling within 480 px;
  - each fire's sweep is clear of terrain;
  - the pit's interior is clear and at least 3.8 Mpx² with its shell;
  - no iron cell has open air above;
  - every terrain cell is a collision tile, a slab or an iron box;
  - each lava basin is closed by terrain at its sides and floor;
  - each `backs` span lies inside an outdoor area.
- `validate_terrain` holds each new family's variants to shared seam pixels (the rocks' outer facets, the cladding's crossing plate) and its decks to 23 px.

### 2.6 The kit change: projectile walls by layer (API sketch)

A projectile meets a wall, a tile (physics layer 0) or a static collider (its `RigidBody::layers`), and chooses by the wall's layers: it passes a wall in its `pass_layers`, turns at one in its `bounce_layers` while `bounced < bounces`, and ends at any other. Today's behaviour is the default: every layer bounces while bounces are left, none passes.

```rust
impl Projectile {
    /// The projectile, turning only at walls in `layers` (one bit per
    /// physics layer, tiles in layer 0) while it has bounces left, and
    /// ending at others. Every layer by default.
    pub fn with_bounce_layers(self, layers: u32) -> Self;
    /// The projectile, passing walls in `layers` as if they were open.
    /// None by default.
    pub fn with_pass_layers(self, layers: u32) -> Self;
    pub fn bounce_layers(&self) -> u32;
    pub fn pass_layers(&self) -> u32;
}

/// The wall a projectile met: its entity (`None` for a tile), its
/// outward normal and its physics layers.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq)] #[non_exhaustive]
pub struct WallContact { pub entity: Option<Entity>, pub normal: Vec2, pub layers: u32 }
impl WallContact { pub fn new(entity: Option<Entity>, normal: Vec2, layers: u32) -> Self; }

/// A projectile turned at a wall in its bounce layers.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq)] #[non_exhaustive]
pub struct ProjectileBounced { pub entity: Entity, pub position: Vec2, pub wall: WallContact }
/// A projectile passed a wall in its pass layers.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq)] #[non_exhaustive]
pub struct ProjectilePassed { pub entity: Entity, pub position: Vec2, pub wall: WallContact }
// ProjectileExpired (already #[non_exhaustive]) gains `pub wall: Option<WallContact>`:
// the wall it ended at, `None` at a hit or the range's end; `new` keeps its
// signature and sets `None`, and `ProjectileExpired::at_wall` builds one with it.
```

- **Order.** In a step the sweep meets walls in order of their fractions: each passed wall sends `ProjectilePassed` and the sweep goes on; the first wall not passed turns or ends it. Hits on hurtboxes before that wall land first, as today; a pierce count and a wall pass are independent.
- **Data.** The scene form takes `"bounce_layers": [0, 2]` and `"pass_layers": [3]`, lists of layer indices 0–31 as `rigid_body`'s `layers` are; the saved form keeps both masks, defaulting to every layer and none, so saves without them load as today's behaviour. The description and the components snapshot list the two fields and the two new events.
- **Plumbing.** `ProjectilePlugin` registers `EventQueue<ProjectileBounced>` and `EventQueue<ProjectilePassed>`; the four new names join the root re-exports and the prelude; every new public item has rustdoc (the crate denies missing docs); no new trace kind and no cue event (a cue event for the meetings is a backlog row).
- **Surface for the freeze.** Additive: new methods, two events, `WallContact`, one field on a `#[non_exhaustive]` event. No break-ledger row.
