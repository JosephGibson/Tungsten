## 2. Design

### 2.1 The slope casts at an exact contact (known issues, Step 1)

- `clip` keeps every side as a bound, so nothing outside the sum is met. When the side a path enters by last is masked and an exposed side's entry lies within the tie's tolerance before it along the path (1e-3 px plus `4 · f32::EPSILON` times the largest coordinate in play, Q1), a tie rounding split, the path enters through that exposed side at the later `t` (`entered_normal`'s rule for a tie); ties between exposed sides, and every path whose last entry is exposed, are unchanged, so a cell with every side exposed returns today's bits (Q1).
- `box_sweep` takes the cell's face mask and enters as `cast` does, so the safety net meets the face; it keeps its one-way rule. Rays and circle casts share `clip` and take the same rule.
- The known issues' prescription, clipping by the exposed sides alone, would drop the masked sides as bounds: a box of half 0.1 from (−10, 10) to (0.5, 0.5) would meet a unit `45 br` with its left and bottom masked at (−1.6, 2.4), on its face's line outside the cell (round 1's 1.1).

### 2.2 Impulses and the push record (P7-2's physics part, Step 2)

- `physics::apply_impulse(world, entity, impulse) -> bool`: a dynamic body's `Velocity` changes by `impulse · inv_mass` at once and its island wakes; a kinematic body with a mass `M` keeps `impulse / M` in its push; a static or kinematic body without a mass, a sensor, a missing body or a non-finite impulse takes none and returns false.
- `physics::apply_radial_impulse(world, center, radius, impulse, filter) -> usize`: each dynamic or kinematic body the filter sees whose shape lies within `radius` of `center` at the last step's positions (`overlap`'s order, `Shape::distance`) takes `impulse · (1 − d / radius)` from `center` toward its centre, up where they coincide, through `apply_impulse`; it returns how many took one. Falloff is linear (Q12); its candidates go through a buffer kept in `PhysicsBuffers`, so a call allocates nothing once warm.
- A kinematic body takes a mass (`RigidBody::with_mass`, `rigid_body.mass`, refused on static bodies and sensors), its reaction mass; the solver still holds it immovable.
- **The push record.** While a kinematic body has a mass, the step keeps a begin rule like the contact record's (A4) for the pairs of a dynamic body and such a body, whether or not a game registered a contact queue. Its closing speeds are sampled where the relax has stripped the bias: at each build and after each relax, before restitution; never after the biased solve or in the arrival pass, whose velocities carry push-out (A18). A pair touches as the record says, an arrival clamp against the kinematic body included (A5); a pair the step does not solve keeps its state as the record does (`D-229`'s Q5). At the step's end, a pair that touched this step and did not the last adds `m · u / (m + M)` to the kinematic body's push, along the first touch's normal into it, `u` the largest sample since it came within reach or last touched at a step's end and `m` the dynamic body's mass; a pair that touched clears its sample. A hit pushes once by its approach, never above its closing speed whatever the restitution; rest and weight push nothing, and a chain reaches the kinematic body through the touching body's own approach at those samples, which may be little. Push-out is stripped as far as the one relax pass strips it: a pair alone converges, while a confined chain, a body pushed out of an overlap whose partner rests on a wall, can keep a residue of at most `max_push_speed`, so push-out alone pushes at most `m · max_push_speed / (m + M)` (Q3, round 2's 2.1).
- `physics::take_push(world, entity) -> Vec2` returns the push kept since the last call and clears it; a despawned body's push, or one that lost its mass, drops at the next step. The state sits in `StepExtra` (`D-229`), and a world without a kinematic mass runs none of it.

### 2.3 The controller on slopes, its drop and its filter (S-99, Step 3)

From 7.8's design (its §2.12), the stopped patch the starting point:

- `with_slope_limit(degrees)` and data `slope_limit`: a surface within it is a floor, a steeper one a wall; left out, the −0.7 rule exactly. `with_slide_limit(degrees)` and `slide_limit` (by default the slope limit): grounded on a slope face steeper than it, it slides down along the face at gravity's share along it, its run added, until the floor is gentler.
- Grounded on a slope tile's face (`SpatialHit::slope`, new), its run moves along the face's tangent at `run · run_speed`; elsewhere along x, today's bits.
- Snap-down: grounded and not launched, its probe reaches `max(1, run_speed · dt · tan(slope limit))` px and snaps onto a floor within it when that floor is a slope face or it stood on one; within 1 px otherwise, so it leaves a flat ledge at today's step. `floor_normal()` reads its last ground's normal.
- `drop_through()`, or a jump press with the Intent's movement down (`y > 0.5`) on a one-way edge, makes its probe and moves skip one-way tiles (`SpatialFilter::with_one_way(false)`, new) until its bottom is below that edge's top, it lands on another floor or it rises; the press does not jump, and the saved form keeps the edge while a drop lasts.
- S-99: `mask` and `without` in its data, numbers or the manifest's `physics_layers` names checked at load (`MissingAsset`), build its filter as `with_mask` and `without_layers` do; the saved form writes their bits when they differ from the defaults, so an older save loads with today's filter. `SpatialFilter::mask()` and `without()` read them; `with_filter` stays for code. The number-or-name list and its resolver are a kit helper, `foundation/layers.rs`, which Step 7 reuses.
- `slide.rs` gains a floor threshold in new functions the old ones call with −0.7, so the top-down mover and the knockback keep today's rule.
- 01's migration comparison sets aside `mask` beside `shove` and `step_height` (A15): one line, no outcome changed.

### 2.4 The controller pushed and riding (K7, K30, S-132, Step 4)

- K7: a controller whose body has a mass takes `take_push` at each step and adds it as a push (A6), so a ball that hits it and a radial impulse move it, and `pushed()` reads what its last step took, for a game that turns a hard hit into a `knock_back`; the ball keeps the solver's stop. Without a mass, nothing (Q4, Q14).
- K30: under locked input, with its body's mass `M`, a dynamic body of mass `m` met as a wall that it cannot shove takes `J = M m / (M + m) · max(0, u − w)` along the move through `apply_impulse`, `u` and `w` the controller's and the body's speeds into the wall, and the controller keeps `u − J / M` rather than 0. Without a mass, or at a static or kinematic body or a tile, it stops as today.
- S-132: a kinematic ground's carry moves by a slide from where its own move ended, the ground refused, so a wall or a ceiling stops it; an unblocked carry moves by `carry · dt` exactly, as today.

All three are inert where no controller's body has a mass and no carry is cut short, as in 01 today, whose elevator holds its trip until its riders stand clear (`examples/01_platformer/src/features/level/elevator.rs:8-12`).

### 2.5 Moving platforms push the controller out (K23, Step 5)

- Before its move, each kinematic body its filter sees overlapping its shape at the last step's positions, but its ground and a body a kit mover drives (`PlatformerController`, `TopDownMover`), pushes it out along their contact's normal by the overlap and the skin, cast against the world with that body refused (`cast_shape_where`); a wall or floor that cuts the push short leaves it overlapped, and `crushed()` reads true for that step. The overlaps go through a buffer the system keeps (Q5). It sees the platform a step late, as every controller cast does.
- It acts in 01, whose four moving platforms and elevator are kinematic bodies its walkers and player may overlap, though 01 sets its player out of the elevator's deck itself (`examples/01_platformer/src/features/level/elevator.rs:14-21`). So it stands alone: 01's tests gate it (§6), and no other row rides on its step.

### 2.6 Top-down movers (K20, S-134, S-92, Step 6)

- `TopDownMover::push(velocity)`: added to the velocity it moves by at its next step and cast with it, so walls stop it; with a rate it joins the kept velocity, which the rates move back toward the target; without one it lasts that step, so a pull is a push each step. A pending push saves, left out at zero.
- `Knockback::with_stacking(true)` and data `stacks` (false by default): a push adds to the one still running, its value now plus the new, its seconds restarting, where the knockback's system runs the push (a bodiless or kinematic entity); a controller's launch and a dynamic body's kick stay as today (Q6). `Knockback::resist()` and `seconds()` read its data.
- S-92: on an entity with a `TopDownMover`, steering writes the mover's direction, its velocity over the mover's speed held to length 1, instead of moving the entity, and reads the mover's last velocity as its own, so the mover's casts stop it at walls. While steering drives it, the mover takes that velocity at once, its own rates set aside, so steering's acceleration and its arrive braking own the motion and its last approach still lands on the point (Q7); `STEERING` runs before `TOP_DOWN_MOVER`. `Steering::set_target(target)` takes a `Target::point` the game sets.

### 2.7 Pickups with bodies and pickups that fall (K19, Step 7)

- The magnet bounds the move it composes. A pulled kinematic pickup is the magnet's alone: its `Velocity` is set toward the collector at the magnet speed, held to the distance over the step, replacing another driver's for that step, so it never passes the collector. A pulled dynamic pickup's `Velocity` is set the same way at each step it is pulled; gravity acts within the step, so it passes the collector along the pull by at most `g · gravity_scale · dt²` (`physics/step/mod.rs:1384-1418`), and physics carries it between. A bodiless or static pickup keeps today's rule, added after the oscillator.
- `Pickup::with_fall(speed)`, data `fall` (0, none, by default) and `fall_layers` (Step 3's numbers or names, every layer by default): a bodiless pickup no magnet pulls falls at `speed` onto the first tile or static body under its shape in those layers, a cast refusing every other body, stopping the controllers' skin short of it so its next cast starts clear (the known issues' `start_overlap` row). The two settings save, left out at their defaults.

### 2.8 Impact damage and bodies as walls (P7-2's kit part, Step 8)

- `ImpactDamage`, scene name `impact_damage`, on a dynamic body or a kinematic one with a mass: `{ "speed": 400, "per_mass": 0.002, "most": 120, "floor": 2, "kind": "<damage type>", "filter": { … } }`. At each `ContactBegan` between its body and an entity with `Health` whose tags pass `filter`, at a closing speed `u` of at least `speed`, it queues a `DamageRequest` of `min(per_mass · m · (u − speed), most)`, none under `floor`, `m` its body's mass, of `kind` when set (a game's type, checked at load), credited to its `Source` when it has one; once per begin. Its system, in the overlap phase, registers `EventQueue<ContactBegan>` the first step a world holds one, so a world without impact damage keeps the record off (`D-233`, Q8).
- `Projectile::with_body_walls(layers)`, data `body_walls` (layer numbers as its other lists, none by default): a kinematic or dynamic body in one of those layers is a wall, met over its own move from `PrevPosition` to `Position` against the sweep, turning or ending the projectile as a static wall does and in the same order with its hits and passes; a bounce reflects in the wall's frame, `v − 2 ((v − w) · n) n`. Without `body_walls`, today's walls.
