## 2. Design

### 2.1 What makes it exact

- A fragment whose alpha is 0 leaves the target as it was under `ALPHA_BLENDING`, and one whose alpha is 1 replaces colour and alpha whatever lay there (A8). So a frame's pixels are, at each pixel, the last opaque fragment in draw order and the fragments after it: a fragment before it in draw order can go unshaded, and a clear one can be dropped.
- **Solid** names a batch every fragment of which is clear or opaque: each instance samples only texels of alpha 0 or 255 through a nearest sampler, at a colour alpha of 255, on the stock or the lit pipeline. A nearest sample is a texel as it is, so the fragment's alpha is exactly 0 or 1 wherever the tint's 255 reaches the fragment as 1, which it does on an interpolator that returns a constant as itself; the pixel tests of §2.4 hold that on the machine they run on.
- **Depth is the list's order.** Batch `i` of `n` draws at depth `1 − (i + 1) / (n + 1)`, strictly lower for a later batch, set by the viewport's depth range (`min = max`), which maps any `z_norm` in the clip range onto it (A9). No vertex, UV or interpolant changes, so every fragment that survives is the fragment the painter drew.
- **Order.** Solid batches first, the last of them first, with `LessEqual` and a depth write, through a fragment entry that drops a clear texel before it writes (`fs_solid`); then every other batch in the list's order, tested and writing nothing. A blend fragment draws exactly when no solid batch after it in the list holds an opaque texel there; an opaque fragment exactly when no later one does; inside one batch the instances draw in order at one depth, as they always did.
- **The clip** (Step 6) is a scissor: it removes fragments and moves none. A batch clipped to the rows its sprite draws loses only fragments that sample wholly clear rows.
- **A light's term is exactly zero out of its reach:** its attenuation is `clamp(1 − d / r, 0, 1)`, 0 wherever `d ≥ r`. The range test (Step 1) skips the term a fragment at a time; the skip (Step 2) for every fragment of a quad the light lies farther from than its radius and what the two stages can round (§2.3).
- What is not claimed: an implementation whose interpolator returns 0.99999994 for a tint of 1 blends an opaque texel with one part in sixteen million of what lies behind, today and after; the two then differ only where a hidden layer's colour could tip an 8-bit rounding. The reference machine's stills are equal. The skip's slack bounds rounding and does not prove it: it holds while the positions the two stages compute and a fragment reads stay within a ten-thousandth of their coordinates and a two-thousandth of the quad's size of the true ones, some 840 float steps, which a seeded test holds it against (A24); the turn's own error it measures.

### 2.3 G1 on `gpu`: the lit path (Steps 1 and 2)

**A fragment skips a light it lies out of reach of** (Step 1, the first plan's stopped step).

- In the point branch of `lit_sprite.wgsl` the attenuation is computed first, exactly as today, and the direction, the normal's dot and the sum run under `if (attenuation > 0.0)`. Core's light-lines test gains the `if` line; `DESIGN.md`'s lighting paragraph gains its sentence, written by hand (A1).

- The row's lit path is expected at about 1.13 ms after it (A3), its target 0.869.

**A quad skips the lights that cannot reach it** (Step 2).

- The rejection is not free (A3): each packed light a fragment lies out of reach of still costs its distance, a division and a compare, 41 % of a term.
- The lit shader's vertex stage marks, a bit a packed light in a flat `skip`, each point light that lies farther from the quad's box than its radius and a slack. The distance is the light's from the quad itself, whatever its turn: the light's offset from the quad's centre is turned back by the sine and cosine that turned the corners and measured against the half sizes. No test a quad can make is tighter; a circle about the quad, which round 1 probed, keeps a lamp that stands beside a long strip (the critique's 2.3). The fragment's loop passes over a marked light before it loads it.
- **The slack bounds what the two stages round** (the critique's 2.1): `(|cx| + |cy|) × 0.0001 + (hx + hy + r) × 0.004`, from the quad's centre and half sizes and the light's radius, taken as at least the shader's minimum. The first term is some 840 float steps of the coordinates, which is how positions round. The second is the turn's: a sine and cosine that are off turn the corners and the offset alike, so their error cancels but for the scale `c² + s²` it gives the quad, and the stage marks nothing unless that scale lies within 0.3 % of 1, which Vulkan's floor for the two (2^-11 each) and Direct3D's (0.0008) both keep. A marked light's attenuation is then exactly 0 at every position a fragment of the quad reads, so the mark removes only terms the range test would have skipped.
- **Held by a CPU mirror** of the stage's lines in the renderer's lighting tests, the lines pinned as core's are: over 32,768 seeded quads and lights no marked light lights a position of its quad, and each part of the slack has a control that fails without it (A24); named cases hold the critique's coordinates, the box against its circle, and the turn's bound.
- **MSAA.** A multisampled fragment's position is its pixel's centre, which can lie outside the quad its samples cover, where a light just short of the quad could still reach. The mark is a shader `override`, `skip_lights`, which the lit pipeline sets by its sample count: on at 1, off above, where the shader draws as after Step 1.
- The lighting block's bind group becomes visible to the vertex stage (A4). No instance field, vertex layout, uniform or CPU code changes; a hot reload rebuilds the pipeline with the same constant.
- **A lit shader file must declare the override,** used or not: this session's build draws a file that declares it and never reads it (`shaders/override-unused.wgsl`). A file without it fails its pipeline's validation and the engine keeps its compiled-in shader, as it does for any shader file that fails (A4): no file of the repository but the engine's own is a lit shader, and a game that ships one adds a line (Q13).
- **Checked by:** the mirror's three tests; the six stills; `just visual`, whose bench fixture an over-eager mark fails (A4) and whose MSAA cases run the mark off; `just smoke`'s lighting and `msaa` rows.
- **`gpu`'s end.** `done` at 0.869 ms or under: this session reads 0.817 (A4), 0.052 under. Over it, the row is split on the step's build before the knock is restored: `bash perf-runs/20261122-gpu-pass-2-plan/tools/g1_split.sh perf-runs/715-gpu-pass-2/step-2` swaps in the step's shader with no term taken, checks that the loader drew it (A4) and puts the shader back; `python3 -B perf-runs/20261122-gpu-pass-2-plan/tools/g1_split.py perf-runs/715-gpu-pass-2/step-2/g1.log perf-runs/715-gpu-pass-2/step-2/reject.log` prints the verdict. `stopped` only where a floor under what any skip of a light leaves reads over the target by itself: the lit shader's cost with no light and the terms' own arithmetic for the fragments in reach. ### 2.4 The depth sort (Steps 3 and 4)

**A sprite's alpha, measured when it loads** (Step 3).

- `tungsten_core::SpriteAlpha`: `Opaque` (every texel 255), `Cutout` (every texel 0 or 255) or `Blend` (some texel between, or never measured), with `SpriteAlpha::measure(rgba)` and `SpriteAlpha::solid(self, filter)`, true for a two-level sprite under a nearest sampler. `SpriteAsset` gains `pub alpha`, `Blend` at `register_sprite`; `AssetRegistry::set_sprite_alpha(id, alpha)` moves `sprite_epoch` when the class changes.
- **New pixels are unmeasured.** `update_sprite_entry`, the public call that puts new pixels under an entry, sets the class back to `Blend`, so a caller that replaces an entry and measures nothing marks nothing solid; the loader measures straight after it.
- The loader measures at its three sites that hold a sprite's pixels: the pack at a load, the in-place reload and the repack (A10); a sheet's frames are measured a frame. The engine's white texel is `Opaque`. A sprite registered without pixels stays `Blend`, so the harness's and a headless test's batches are never solid unless the test sets the class.
- One pass over the decoded bytes, with no early exit and no branch on the data: measured on `boot` in the step's sitting.

**Solid batches first** (Step 4).

- `SpriteBatch` gains `pub solid: bool`, false from `SpriteBatch::new`, with §2.1's contract in its doc. Under `cpu_stable` it is inert.
- **The renderer under `gpu_depth`.** The sprite and lit pipelines are built twice: the tested one (`fs_main`, `LessEqual`, no write) and the solid one (`fs_solid`, `LessEqual`, write); a material's pipeline tests and never writes, its shader promising nothing. `SpritePipeline::draw` takes the pass's target size, orders the frame by `draw_turns` (solid batches reversed, then the rest in order; a batch whose material draws it is never solid, a lit one may be, `D-061`), and sets each batch's depth by `batch_depth` through the viewport, the whole range again when it returns. The turns and the batches' first instances live in the pipeline between frames, so the draw allocates nothing new.
- **`fs_solid`.** In `sprite.wgsl`: sample, `discard` where the texel's own alpha is under 0.5, else the texel times the tint, as `fs_main`. In `lit_sprite.wgsl` the lit colour moves into a function both entries call with the three samples, taken before the `discard` so that every sample stays in uniform control flow; a clear texel of a lit cutout no longer runs the light loop. A hot reload rebuilds both pipelines of a pair or neither.
- **`z_norm`** stays in the instance and in the extracts as they write it; no stock pipeline's depth reads it under either sort. `Extracts`' and the sprite extract's docs and the sprite shader's comment say so.
- **The scene depth target** is allocated only when the pass attaches it (`gpu_depth` with depth enabled), which ends known issues' F11 half (A6).
- **Who marks a batch.** The tile extract: a layer's batch is solid while every tile in it is, its sprite `solid` under its filter and the layer's tint at alpha 255; no batch is split, so `sprite_batches` and the tiles' order stand. The engine's backdrops: backdrops that follow one another on one page share a batch while they agree on being solid, so a cloud bank no longer takes the sky's mark with it; unmeasured sprites share as before. The `Sprite` entities' batches are never marked (non-goal): a batch there holds a class's sprites of one z-run, and marking it wants a sprite's class in the row loop that 7.14 tuned.
- **Pixel tests** (`just visual`, local), with A21's controls: the bench's `visual` preset under `gpu_depth` against `cpu_stable`, tolerance 0, at four endings (the preset, the scene alone, MSAA 4, SMAA), which fails on the renderer before this step and holds the tested path; and the fixtures' `seams` sheet under both sorts, every pixel equal, which holds the solid batches' order.
- **The decision** (the tungsten-decision skill, the next free ID): `gpu_depth` draws the painter's picture, a batch's depth its place in the list; a solid batch's contract and who may mark one; a sprite's alpha measured at decode and dropped with its pixels. It names the M43 finding it ends.
- **The renderer's rules** gain one clause, the file trimmed elsewhere to stay under its cap (A23): under `gpu_depth` a batch's depth is its place and solid batches draw first, and `fs_solid` must draw what `fs_main` draws.
